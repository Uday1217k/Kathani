# The Rhythmic Heartbeat

**Genre:** Romance | **Date:** 2026-07-29

### Characters
*   Leo: A quiet, orderly software developer.
*   Chloe: A creative graphic designer, lives in the apartment above Leo.

### The Story
Leo loved his apartment. It was small, but it was his quiet place. He worked from home most days, staring at lines of code, needing calm to think. Then Chloe moved in upstairs. She wasn't loud in the usual way, no booming music or big parties. It was worse. It was a *thump*. A soft, rhythmic thump, like a slow drumbeat, that started around six every evening and lasted for an hour or so.

Thump-thump. Thump-thump. Thump-thump.

It drove Leo crazy. He tried earplugs. He tried listening to his own music. He tried to ignore it, but the sound seeped into his brain. He imagined her moving furniture, or maybe even clumsily practicing some sort of dance. He pictured someone completely careless, just dropping things on the floor with no thought for the person below.

One night, after a particularly long thumping session, Leo snapped. He got up from his desk, marched to his door, and stomped up the stairs. He took a deep breath, trying to calm his fast-beating heart, and knocked.

The door opened a crack. A woman with bright, curious eyes and a messy bun peered out. “Yes?” she asked, a small smile on her face. Her voice was soft.

Leo felt a bit silly, standing there with his complaint. “Hi,” he started, clearing his throat. “I’m Leo. I live downstairs. Look, I don’t want to be rude, but there’s this… sound. Every evening. A thumping noise. It’s pretty constant. I was just wondering if you could, maybe, keep it down?” He finished, feeling a rush of heat in his cheeks.

The woman’s smile faded. Her cheeks went a little pink. “Oh,” she said, her voice small. “The thumping. I’m so sorry. I didn’t think it was loud enough to bother anyone.” She pushed the door open wider. Her apartment was bright, filled with plants and art. In the middle of the living room, on a clean, soft rug, sat a mountain of fabric pieces and a big bowl of lentils.

She pointed to the bowl. “It’s these,” she said, her voice a whisper. “I make stress balls. For the local hospital’s children’s ward. The kids squeeze them when they’re scared. I fill these little pouches,” she picked up a small, colourful fabric shape, “with the lentils, and then I have to… pat them. Like this.” She gently patted the little pouch. Thump-thump. Thump-thump.

Leo stared, mouth slightly open. The sound, the annoying, frustrating sound, was *her* gently patting lentil-filled stress balls. His imagined image of a careless, clumsy neighbor vanished. He saw only her, looking a little embarrassed, surrounded by colourful fabric and lentils, making soft, comforting things for kids.

### Conclusion
“Oh,” Leo said, his own cheeks burning now. “I… I see. I’m so sorry. I work from home, and I guess I just focused on it too much.” He felt terrible. “Do you… do you need any help?” Chloe looked up, her bright eyes meeting his. A new, shy smile spread across her face. “Actually,” she said, “a second pair of hands would make them even better.” Leo found himself smiling back, stepping into her apartment, the soft, rhythmic thump of lentils suddenly sounding not like an annoyance, but like a gentle, welcoming heartbeat.
