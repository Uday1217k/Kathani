# The Bench Agreement

**Genre:** Romance | **Date:** 2026-09-23

### Characters
*   Leo: A young man who loves reading in the park.
*   Clara: A young woman who likes sketching in the park.

### The Story
Leo had a favorite spot. It was a wooden bench, a bit worn, under a big oak tree in the town park. He liked to read there. He often saw a girl there too, Clara. She sat on the same bench, but usually on the other end, sketching in a notebook. She had bright eyes and a quick smile, even when just to herself. Leo often thought about saying hello, but the words never came out right.

One sunny Tuesday, Leo had an idea. He finished his book, a thick paperback, and slowly packed his bag. Then, as if by accident, he left the book on the bench. Inside, tucked between the pages, was a small, folded piece of paper. On it, he had written: "This is my favorite book. I hope whoever finds it enjoys it as much as I did. This is my favorite bench too." He walked away, not looking back, but his heart thumped a little.

The next day, Leo went back. The book was still there. But next to it, tucked under a loose page, was another note. It was written in neat, curly letters. "Found your favorite book on my favorite bench! It's a good read. Thanks for sharing. - C." Leo smiled. So she *had* found it. He wrote back: "Glad you liked it! I was worried someone else might take it. Do you often sit here? - L." He left both notes inside the book and placed it back on the bench.

This started their quiet game. Clara would leave a note, often about something simple. "Saw a squirrel trying to steal a sandwich today. Made me laugh. - C." Leo would reply, "The squirrels here are bold! I saw a lady share her lunch with one yesterday. - L." They never saw each other leaving the notes. They would only find them.

One morning, Leo found a small, bright blue pen on the bench. It was a pretty pen, covered in tiny stars. He knew it wasn't his. He picked it up. He wondered if it belonged to 'C'. He wrote a note: "Found a starry pen. Is it yours, 'C'? It's very nice. - L." He left it with the pen.

The next day, the pen was gone. A new note was in its place. "Yes! My favorite pen! Thank you, L! I was so sad to lose it. How did you know it was mine? - C." Leo chuckled. He hadn't truly known, just guessed. He wrote back, "Just a feeling. Maybe because your notes are always so bright, like stars. - L."

Weeks went by. The notes grew longer, more personal. They talked about their jobs (Leo worked at a small bookshop, Clara was a freelance graphic designer). They shared small worries and big dreams. They learned about each other's favorite ice cream flavors and silly childhood memories. They knew so much about the other, yet they still hadn't really met. They only knew the person from the bench notes.

One sunny afternoon, Clara's note said: "L, I feel like I know you so well, but I don't really know you at all. How about we finally meet? Tomorrow, same bench, 3 PM? If you want to, of course. - C." Leo's heart jumped. He quickly wrote back: "Yes! 3 PM. Can't wait! - L."

### Conclusion
The next day, Leo arrived at the bench a little early. He saw Clara already sitting there. She was looking at her watch, a small smile on her face. She looked up, and their eyes met. "L?" she asked, a soft laugh in her voice. "C?" he replied, feeling a wide grin spread across his face. He walked closer. "You know," she said, her smile getting wider, "I saw you leave that book the first time. I knew it wasn't really lost." Leo stopped. His mouth fell open a little. "You did?" he asked. "Yes," she nodded. "And the pen? I knew you'd find it." Leo blinked. Then he started to laugh. "I left the book on purpose," he confessed, "hoping you'd pick it up." Clara laughed too. "And I left the pen on purpose, hoping you'd notice," she admitted. They both looked at each other, then back at the worn wooden bench, the silent witness to their clever, sweet game.
