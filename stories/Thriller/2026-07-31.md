# The Daily List

**Genre:** Thriller | **Date:** 2026-07-31

### Characters
Clara Jensen: A quiet librarian.
Mr. Finch: Clara's new, very private neighbor.

### The Story
Clara loved her routine. Every morning, she made coffee, watered her fern, and left for the library. Simple. Safe. Until the first list appeared.

It was on her porch, folded neatly. "7:00 AM - Coffee. 7:30 AM - Leave for work. 8:00 AM - Arrive Library." Her heart jumped. It was *her* morning. Who knew?

The next day, a similar list was taped to her mailbox. "12:00 PM - Lunch break. 1:00 PM - Return to desk. 5:00 PM - Close up." Every step of her day, written down. Clara felt a cold knot in her stomach.

She started looking around. Her new neighbor, Mr. Finch, moved in last month. He was a quiet man, always seemed to be out on his own porch, reading. But now, Clara noticed he was often watching her. A quick glance over his book, a stare from his window.

The lists kept coming. On her car windshield. Tucked under her door mat. Always short, always accurate, always about her. Clara stopped sleeping well. Her simple life felt like a cage. She was being watched. She was sure it was Mr. Finch.

One afternoon, she saw him leave in his small car. This was her chance. Her hands shook as she found the spare key he kept under a flowerpot, just like she did. The lock clicked.

Inside, his apartment was bare. No pictures, no clutter. Just a neat living room, a kitchen, and a closed bedroom door. On his small table sat a single, thick notebook. Clara picked it up.

Her blood ran cold. The notebook was filled, page after page, with her life. Her routines, her habits, her small actions. More detailed than any list she'd received. "Clara hums while making coffee. Buys oat milk on Tuesdays. Reads mystery novels." It was all there. He wasn't just observing her; he was studying her.

Then she heard it. The faint rumble of a car engine getting closer. Mr. Finch was back. Clara panicked, shoving the notebook back onto the table. She darted into the bedroom, pulling the door almost shut.

She watched through the crack. Mr. Finch came in, his shoulders slumped. He looked tired. He walked straight to the table, saw the notebook, and frowned. Then his eyes fell on something else. A piece of paper, folded, lay right beside the notebook.

He picked it up, unfolded it. His eyes scanned the words. A deep sigh escaped him. "Not again," he muttered, his voice raspy. He ran a hand through his hair. "I tried. I really did."

He looked up, directly at the bedroom door. Clara froze, her breath caught in her throat. Had he seen her?

Mr. Finch didn't move. He just stared at the door, his eyes wide and scared. He held the paper closer, as if reading it again. His lips moved silently. Then, with a slow, deliberate motion, he pinned the paper to his bare wall, right next to the door.

Clara could just make out the plain, blocky writing.
It was another list. Not for Clara. Not about Clara's day.
This one was for Mr. Finch.

### Conclusion
"You failed to deliver 'Day 5' list. You were observed. Consequences will follow. Your next instruction will arrive tomorrow, 7:00 AM, at Clara's porch." Mr. Finch dropped his head into his hands, a single tear tracing a path down his cheek. Clara, hidden in the dark room, finally understood. Mr. Finch wasn't the one watching her. He was just the messenger, trying to avoid his own bitter instructions, and failing. They were both trapped in someone else's twisted game.
