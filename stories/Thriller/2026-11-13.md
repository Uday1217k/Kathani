# The Hidden Story

**Genre:** Thriller | **Date:** 2026-11-13

### Characters
Sarah: A quiet woman who cleans offices at night.
Mr. Davies: The office building manager, always watchful.
Eleanor Vance: A desk worker on the third floor, owner of the green journal.

### The Story
Sarah loved her job. Not the cleaning itself, but the quiet of the empty office building. At night, the tall glass tower became hers. She moved through the silent rooms, wiping desks, emptying bins, making everything neat again. It was a simple life, and she liked it that way.

One Tuesday, deep in the old archives section on the lowest floor, Sarah found something odd. She was dusting a rarely used corner, behind a stack of dusty law books. Her hand brushed against a loose panel in an old wooden desk. It gave way easily. Inside the small space, tucked away, was a dark green journal. It looked old, worn, with a faded ribbon marker.

Curiosity got the better of her. She pulled it out. The journal was filled with neat, handwritten words. Sarah flipped it open to a random page. Her eyes landed on a few lines: "...the drop will be at midnight. They won't know I've slipped away. The loose end must be handled, quietly." Sarah’s breath hitched. "Loose end"? This sounded like something from a bad movie. But the handwriting was so real, so careful. She closed the journal quickly, her heart thumping. This was not a diary. This was a plan. She put the journal in her bag. She didn't know why, but she couldn't leave it there.

The next day, everything felt different. Every car parked outside her apartment building looked suspicious. Was that the same dark sedan from yesterday? At work, Mr. Davies, the building manager, stopped her. "Everything alright, Sarah?" he asked, his eyes seeming to linger. "Didn't find anything unusual last night, did you? A few things have gone missing." Sarah felt her stomach clench. He was asking about the journal, she was sure of it. She mumbled something about just doing her job and hurried away.

Later, she looked inside the journal again. A name kept appearing: "Eleanor Vance." She discreetly checked the staff list. Eleanor Vance worked on the third floor, a quiet woman with thick glasses. Sarah had seen her before, packing up her desk late. Eleanor looked so ordinary. Could she be planning something so dark? The note under Sarah's door that evening sealed her fear. It wasn't addressed to her, but it read: "To resident, please keep quiet after 10 PM. We know you're busy, but let's keep the noise down." Sarah read it again. "We know you're busy... keep the noise down." It had to be a warning. They knew she had the journal. They wanted her to stay silent.

Panic took over. Sarah decided she had to act. She saw Eleanor Vance leaving work late, walking to her car. This was it. This was the moment of truth. Sarah followed, staying hidden behind parked cars and bushes. Eleanor drove a few blocks, then pulled into a quiet street near a community hall. She parked, got out, and walked towards a small garden. From her bag, she took out a small wooden box. Sarah’s heart pounded. Was this the "drop"? Was this where the "loose end" would be handled?

Eleanor opened the box. Inside were small, smooth rocks, painted with bright colors and pictures. Eleanor carefully picked one out, a rock with a smiling sun on it, and placed it gently on a stone bench. She then pulled out a small, almost empty journal from her bag, and quickly wrote something in it.

Sarah couldn't take it anymore. She stepped out from behind a large bush. "What are you doing?" she demanded, her voice shaking. "And the green journal? The 'loose end'? What's your plan?"

### Conclusion
Eleanor Vance spun around, startled, dropping her painted rock. Her eyes, wide behind her glasses, looked at Sarah with a mix of shock and confusion, then slowly, a little sadness. "My plan?" Eleanor asked softly. "I'm hiding a kindness rock. It's for people to find. And this journal," she patted the small book in her hand, "is where I write my daily story ideas."

She picked up the rock. "The green one you found... it's my first thriller novel draft. The 'drop at midnight,' the 'loose end' – those are plot points for my story. I work on it during my breaks, then hide it so nobody reads it before it's finished. I get embarrassed easily."

Sarah felt the blood drain from her face. "But... Mr. Davies? The dark car? The note?"

Eleanor smiled gently. "Mr. Davies was asking if anyone saw his stapler, he told me this morning. The dark car belongs to my new neighbor, a lovely man, same model as mine. And the note under your door? That was probably from Mrs. Henderson, two floors up. She keeps sending notes about 'quiet hours' to everyone. She's a bit jumpy."

Sarah stood there, feeling a wave of relief wash over her, followed by a hot flush of embarrassment. The grand, dangerous plot, the shadowy figures, the threats – they were all just creations of her own mind, fueled by a fictional story. The only thriller was the one she had lived, all in her head. Eleanor, the "mastermind," was just a quiet writer, sharing small painted rocks of joy.
