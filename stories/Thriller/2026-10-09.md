# The Usual Place

**Genre:** Thriller | **Date:** 2026-10-09

### Characters
*   Tom: A man in his late 30s, works in an office, prone to forgetfulness.
*   Officer Miller: A local police officer.
*   Mrs. Gable: An elderly lady, a volunteer for a neighborhood charity and watch.

### The Story
Tom noticed the beige sedan a week ago. It parked a few houses down his street, right about the time he left for work. He didn't think much of it at first. People parked on the street all the time. But then it happened every day. Same car, same spot, same time.

He started watching it. From his kitchen window, coffee in hand. The car was always empty. No driver, no passenger. It just sat there. It felt like it was waiting. Or watching. Tom felt a chill. His quiet street, his quiet life, suddenly had an unwelcome shadow.

His mind started playing tricks. He'd hear creaks in the night. See shadows shift when no breeze blew. He double-checked his locks, sometimes triple-checked them. He started leaving for work a few minutes early, hoping to catch the driver. No luck. The car would be there, empty, unmoving.

One morning, Tom decided he'd had enough. He got a clear shot of the license plate with his phone camera. He called the local police, asking about suspicious activity. Officer Miller, a friendly but tired voice, took down the details. "We'll check it out, Mr. Davies. Probably just someone parking there for work nearby."

The next day, the car was gone. Tom felt a wave of relief. Maybe the police had scared them off. He drove to work, feeling lighter. But when he came home, there it was again. Parked in the exact same spot. Tom's heart sank.

He called Officer Miller again. The officer sounded a little more annoyed this time. "Mr. Davies, the car belongs to 'Safe Street Community Outreach'. It's one of their delivery vehicles for meals. The plate is clean. They operate all over town."
Tom felt silly. A charity car? He mumbled an apology. Still, it didn't feel right. Why *his* street, every day, empty?

The next morning, as Tom walked to his car, the beige sedan pulled up beside him. The window rolled down. An old lady with a kind smile looked out. "Morning, dear! You must be Tom Davies. I'm Mrs. Gable. I've been meaning to catch you."
Tom felt his cheeks flush. "Oh. You're... the driver?"
"That's right! Your sister, Lisa, asked me to keep an eye on your place. She's worried about you, said you've been a bit absent-minded lately, forgetting things. We're part of the 'Neighborhood Watch' here. I usually just park and keep an eye on things until my next delivery." Mrs. Gable gave him a warm smile. "Looks like you remembered to lock your door today, good job!"
Tom forced a smile. He felt a huge weight lift from his shoulders. He'd been so paranoid over nothing. He thanked Mrs. Gable and watched her drive off.

He walked up his porch steps, feeling a little foolish but mostly relieved. He reached for his doorknob, then stopped. Mrs. Gable's words echoed in his head: "Looks like you remembered to lock your door today..." But the door wasn't locked. It was slightly ajar, maybe an inch open. He must have forgotten to close it properly this morning. He sighed, shaking his head at his own forgetfulness.

He pushed the door open and stepped inside. The air was still. Everything looked normal. Then he saw it. On his coffee table, a small, sleek black box. It wasn't there when he left this morning. He was sure of it. He picked it up. It felt cold, heavy. A tiny red light blinked on its side. It looked like a recording device.

A shiver ran down his spine. He pressed a button marked 'Play'.
A voice filled the silent room. *His* voice. Low, urgent, and filled with fear.
"Don't trust the car. Don't trust the police. They're coming for it. Find the code. It's in the usual place. Hide. Don't forget again. *Don't forget*."

### Conclusion
The recording ended, leaving Tom alone with the chilling echo of his own voice and the terrible knowledge that *he* was the one who had hidden a secret, and *he* was the one who was forgetting it. The danger wasn't outside, watching him. It was a race against his own fading memory, a desperate plea from a past self he no longer fully knew, warning him about an unknown 'they' who were now closer than ever. The "usual place" suddenly felt like the most terrifying riddle he'd ever faced.
