# The Gallery Game

**Genre:** Thriller | **Date:** 2026-08-07

### Characters
Clara: A quiet, observant gallery assistant.
Mr. Blackwood: The kind, elderly owner of the art gallery.

### The Story
Clara loved her job at the old art gallery. It was usually quiet. She cleaned dusty frames and wrote notes about forgotten art pieces. One Tuesday, she was wiping down a big, dark painting in the storage room. It showed a forest at night. Her cleaning cloth caught on something rough on the back of the frame. It wasn't the wood. It was a faint, small symbol carved into the frame itself. It looked like a wavy line with a dot at the end.

Curiosity pricked her. She looked at the symbol closer. It seemed very old. Not part of the painting, but something added later. She remembered seeing something like it once, in a book about local history. It was a forgotten mark of an old guild, a group of local craftspeople from long ago.

The next day, she found another one. This time, it was on the bottom of a heavy stone statue, in a different room. Again, it was tiny, easy to miss. This symbol was a circle with a small cross inside. Her heart beat a little faster. This wasn't a mistake. Someone had put these symbols here on purpose.

She started looking everywhere. During her breaks, she would secretly check the undersides of display stands, the backs of small carvings. It became a game, a private hunt. She found a third symbol, then a fourth. They were like breadcrumbs. Each symbol, when she thought about it, seemed to point to the next one. A wavy line pointing right, a circle suggesting a turn.

The symbols slowly formed a path. It led her through different rooms of the gallery, always to a new, hidden mark. She felt like a detective in an old movie. But then, a new feeling started. A shiver down her spine.

Mr. Blackwood, the gallery owner, a kind old man, started to seem different. She’d catch his eye sometimes. Was he watching her? Once, she saw him walking past the storage room, just after she had been looking at the forest painting. He smiled, but it felt... knowing.

Then, a small security camera appeared in her section. It was new. She hadn't seen it there before. And her phone rang with no one on the other end, twice in one day. Her quiet game turned scary. Was she uncovering something serious? A secret society? A hidden vault of stolen art? She felt a knot in her stomach. Someone knew what she was doing. They were trying to stop her.

The last symbol was the hardest to find. It was on the underside of a small, wooden bird carving, tucked away on a high shelf. It was an arrow pointing straight down, right at a specific glass display case in the main hall.

Her hands shook a little. This was it. The end of the trail. The big secret. She waited until the gallery closed. Mr. Blackwood usually left a bit later. She pretended to tidy up. When he finally said goodnight and the main door clicked shut, Clara was alone.

The gallery was silent. Her breathing felt loud. She walked to the display case, the one with an ordinary ceramic vase. The final arrow pointed to it. She looked around one last time, making sure she was truly alone.

With trembling hands, she carefully opened the latch on the glass case. She reached inside, lifting the vase. Beneath it, a small, folded piece of paper lay flat against the velvet. Her heart hammered. This was it. The truth.

### Conclusion
She unfolded the paper. It was a note, handwritten. "Clara," it read, "I saw you finding the symbols. I created this 'treasure hunt' for my grandson years ago, but he never found the end. I hoped someone would. It makes me happy to see you enjoy puzzles so much. Please enjoy this small bonus from the gallery for your cleverness." Next to the note was a crisp fifty-dollar bill. Clara stared at the note, then at the money. The camera, Mr. Blackwood's looks, the silent calls... it had all been her own fear, painting a thriller where there was only a kind old man's forgotten game.
