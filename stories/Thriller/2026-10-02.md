# The Birdwatcher

**Genre:** Thriller | **Date:** 2026-10-02

### Characters
Sarah: A young woman living alone in an apartment.
Mr. Finch: An older man, Sarah's neighbor in the building across the street.

### The Story
Sarah spent most days by her window. Her job was on a computer, but her eyes often drifted to the building opposite. It was a normal street, normal windows. But one window always seemed to hold her attention. It belonged to Mr. Finch.

Mr. Finch had moved in a few months ago. Sarah didn't know much about him, just that he was an older man, always quiet. But lately, Sarah felt he was watching her. Not just a quick look, but a long, steady stare. She'd glance up from her screen, and there he'd be, at his window, looking right at her apartment. Even when her blinds were closed, she felt his eyes. It made her skin prickle.

One afternoon, she saw him hold something to his eyes. It was dark, shiny. Binoculars. Her heart jumped. He was definitely watching her. The next day, a small package appeared at her door. It wasn't for her. She didn't open it. Later, when she checked again, the package was gone. She didn't remember seeing anyone. Her nerves tightened into a knot.

She told her friend, Lisa, on the phone. "He watches me, Lisa. With binoculars. And things happen."
Lisa tried to calm her. "Sarah, you've been a bit stressed lately. Maybe he's just an old man who looks out his window. The package could have been a mistake."
But Sarah knew. This was different. She started taking long routes to the grocery store, just to avoid walking past his building. She checked her locks three times before bed.

One night, a faint tapping started on her window. *Tap, tap, tap.* Sarah froze in her bed. It was very soft, but it was there. She lay still, listening. It stopped. Then started again. *Tap, tap, tap.* She pulled her blanket tighter. He was trying to get her attention. He was getting closer.

The next evening, it happened again. A distinct scratching sound at her front door. Sarah's breath hitched. She tiptoed to the door, pressing her eye to the peephole. Her heart hammered against her ribs. Nothing. No one. But on her doormat lay a small, oddly shaped piece of wood. It was rough, like it had been carved. She quickly pulled it inside, her hand shaking. It looked like a tiny, unfinished bird. A warning? A message? Her mind spun with fear. This was too much. She had to act.

### Conclusion
With a burst of courage fueled by terror, Sarah grabbed her phone and ran out. Not to call the police yet, but to confront him, to make him stop. She hurried across the street, her eyes fixed on Mr. Finch's building. Her breath came in short gasps. As she reached his front door, hand raised to ring the bell, a soft, real bird chirp made her pause. She looked up. There, perched on the ledge just above his door, was a small, brown bird. It held a tiny, carved wooden bird in its beak, trying to drop it onto the doorstep. As the bird struggled, it accidentally scraped its claws on the glass of Mr. Finch's window. Sarah stared, then slowly, she looked down at the rough, carved bird in her own hand. The scratching, the tapping, the wooden bird… it clicked into place. Mr. Finch was not a stalker. He was a birdwatcher, and the birds, it seemed, liked to leave him gifts. Sarah glanced at her reflection in the glass of his door – wide, fearful eyes, messy hair. She realized, with a sudden, sinking feeling, that the one watching, the one creating the whole terrifying story, had been herself all along.
