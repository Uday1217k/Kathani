# The Empty Room

**Genre:** Thriller | **Date:** 2026-09-11

### Characters
Sarah: A young woman, new to apartment 3B.
Mr. Henderson: Sarah's elderly neighbor in apartment 3A, seemingly kind.

### The Story
Sarah loved her new apartment. It was on the third floor of an old building, with big windows and a quiet feel. Apartment 3B was her fresh start. She moved in boxes, unpacked her things, and made the small space her own. On her second day, a knock came at her door. It was Mr. Henderson from next door, 3A. He was an older man with kind eyes and a slow smile. He brought a plate of cookies and said he’d lived there for fifty years. "Welcome to the building, dear," he said, "If you ever need anything, just knock." Sarah felt lucky to have such a friendly neighbor.

A few days later, small things started to happen. She’d leave her bedroom lamp off, but find it on later. Her spare key, which always hung by the door, went missing for a full day before she found it tucked under a cushion. She laughed at herself, blaming tiredness or forgetfulness. Mr. Henderson saw her in the hallway. "Settling in okay?" he asked. "You look a little tired, dear. Old buildings can take some getting used to." He seemed to know just what she was feeling.

Then, things felt less like forgetfulness. One night, Sarah woke to a soft creaking sound. Her bedroom door, which she always closed fully, was slightly open. A sliver of light from the living room cut through the darkness. Her heart thumped. She called out, "Hello?" Nothing. She grabbed her phone, her hand shaking. Who would be in her apartment? She slowly got out of bed, peeked into the living room. Everything was still. No one was there. She called Mr. Henderson, feeling silly but too scared to be alone. He came right over, bathrobe and all. He checked every room, even looked under the bed. "Just the old building settling, dear," he said gently, "These places have their own sounds. It's a solid building, nothing to worry about." He stayed until she felt calmer.

He suggested she get a new deadbolt for the front door and maybe a small security camera. "Just for peace of mind," he offered. "I can even help you put them up. I’m good with tools." Sarah agreed, grateful. The next afternoon, they installed a small camera in her living room, pointing towards her front door. It had a little green light when it was on. Mr. Henderson made sure it was set up correctly.

That evening, Sarah felt better. She watched TV, ate dinner, and then went to bed. The next morning, she checked the camera footage. It showed nothing. Just her leaving, then coming back. Yet, when she walked into her living room, her favorite framed photo on the shelf was upside down. She stared at it. The camera was right there! It should have caught anyone touching it. But the video was empty. She felt a cold dread. Was she going crazy? Was someone getting in and out without a trace? The thought made her stomach twist. She called Mr. Henderson, her voice shaking. "Maybe it's time to think about moving, dear," he said, his voice soft. "This place might not be right for you."

Sarah sat on her sofa, surrounded by boxes she now wanted to pack. The afternoon sun streamed in. She felt defeated, ready to give up on her new start. She looked up at the ceiling, then around her living room. Her eyes landed on the small camera she and Mr. Henderson had installed yesterday. It was on, its green light glowing. And then, she saw it. A tiny, almost invisible glint above her front door frame. It was high up, tucked right into the molding, painted the exact same color as the wall. It had a tiny red light.

### Conclusion
It wasn't the camera she and Mr. Henderson had installed. That one faced the door. This tiny red-lit camera was pointing straight into her living room. It had been there long before she moved in. Mr. Henderson hadn't been helping her. He had been watching her. And now, he was just outside her door, waiting for her to leave, so he could finally get to whatever secret had been hidden in apartment 3B all along.
