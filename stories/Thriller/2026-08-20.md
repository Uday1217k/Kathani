# The Clockmaker's Game

**Genre:** Thriller | **Date:** 2026-08-20

### Characters
*   Clara: A librarian, quiet and precise.
*   Mr. Henderson: An old man who runs a small clock repair shop.
*   Two "Men": Large, gruff men.

### The Story
Clara pushed the last book onto its shelf. The old library was quiet. Dust danced in the last bits of sun. It was her turn to close up. She walked past the empty desks, checking doors. All clear.

Then she saw it. A book was out of place on the bottom shelf, near the back wall. Not a book, really. It was a small, dark leather journal. It looked old, very old. And it had a tiny lock on its front. Clara picked it up. It felt heavy, important. But how did it get there? She put it in her bag.

The next day, Clara took the journal to Mr. Henderson's shop. He was an old man, always polite, always smelling of oil and wood. He fixed clocks and things. "Can you open this?" Clara asked. Mr. Henderson put on his glasses. He looked at the lock, then at Clara. His eyes held a strange light. "I can try, Clara. Leave it with me."

Later that afternoon, her phone rang. It was Mr. Henderson. His voice was shaky. "Clara, you need to come back. This journal... it's strange. And someone was just here asking about it. Asking about you." Clara felt a chill. Her heart beat fast. "I'm coming," she said.

When she got to the shop, the lights were off. A small glow came from the back room. Clara pushed the door open a crack. She saw Mr. Henderson. He looked very scared. He waved her in quickly. "Hide, Clara! Now!" he whispered. He pointed to a small closet under the stairs. Clara slid inside. The door did not close all the way, leaving a thin gap.

Then the main shop door opened. Two big men walked in. Their faces were hard. "Where is it?" one of them grunted. "And where is the girl?" Mr. Henderson stammered, "I... I don't know what you mean." The other man laughed, a harsh sound. "The journal. The one from the library. And Clara. She has it." Clara froze. They knew her name.

"I gave it to her," Mr. Henderson said. "She took it home." There was a loud thump, then a shout from Mr. Henderson. "No, please!" Another loud crash, like wood breaking. Then silence. Clara bit her lip. She could not breathe. She heard the men walk around, searching. Then, their footsteps faded. The shop door opened and closed.

Clara waited. It felt like hours. Slowly, she pushed the closet door open. The shop was empty. The counter was messy. Mr. Henderson was gone. Clara’s hands shook. She ran out of the shop, into the street. She called the police from a payphone. "He's gone!" she cried. "They took him!"

The police came. They looked around Mr. Henderson's shop. They found nothing. No broken wood, no signs of a fight. Just a bit of mess. "Are you sure, Miss?" a kind officer asked. "There's nothing here." Clara felt crazy. She insisted she heard it all. But they just looked at her with pity. She still had the journal in her bag.

### Conclusion
Clara went home. She felt weak. She pulled out the old journal. It was still locked. She looked at the tiny lock. It was simple. She found a hairpin and tried to pick it. After a few tries, she heard a small click. The journal opened.

It was not filled with secrets or names. It was a child’s diary. Full of simple drawings of flowers, cats, and stick figures. One drawing showed a small shop, with a happy old man standing outside. Underneath, in messy, child-like writing, it said: "Mr. Henderson's secret hideout."

Clara turned to the very last page. There, a note was written in neat, adult handwriting. It said: "Dear Clara, Did you enjoy the show? Welcome to the game. P.S. Look behind you."

A soft *tick-tock* sounded behind her. Clara spun around. Mr. Henderson stood in her doorway, holding an old, smiling clock. His eyes, the same ones from the shop, sparkled. He was not hurt. He was not scared. He just smiled. "That," he said, "was just Act One."
