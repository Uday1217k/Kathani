# The Keeper's Gifts

**Genre:** Thriller | **Date:** 2026-09-25

### Characters
Lena: A woman living alone, in her early thirties, who values her quiet life.

### The Story
Lena liked her quiet apartment. Every morning, she made toast and coffee. Every evening, she read a book. Her life was neat, predictable. Then, the packages started coming.

The first one was a plain brown box. The label said "To: The Keeper." Lena checked the address. It was hers. She looked at her mail slot. No name "The Keeper" lived here. Lena thought it was a mistake. She called the post office. They said they delivered it as addressed. She opened it. Inside was a small, broken locket. It was old, tarnished. Lena shrugged. Someone's junk mail, she guessed. She put it in the bin.

A week later, another box arrived. Same plain brown, same address: "To: The Keeper." This time, inside was a single dried flower. It looked like a daisy, but brittle and grey with age. Lena felt a prickle of unease. Who was "The Keeper"? And why were they sending her old things? She held the flower. It gave off a faint, musty smell. She felt a shiver.

She started locking her door twice. She checked her windows at night. The packages kept coming, once a week, like clockwork. One box held a crumpled, faded letter, its words rubbed away. Another had a child's drawing, just scribbles of bright crayon on torn paper. Lena found herself staring at the mail slot, waiting for the next one. She felt like someone was watching her. She walked faster on her way home. She turned on all the lights in her apartment.

One evening, after she'd found a small, old music box in a package, she decided enough was enough. She pulled out the phone book. She looked up "private investigators." Her finger hovered over a number. But what would she tell them? "Someone is sending me old junk addressed to 'The Keeper'?" They'd laugh.

She put the phone down. Instead, she went to her kitchen. She pulled out the trash bin. She dug through the coffee grounds and old food wrappers. She found the first box, the one with the broken locket. Then the second, with the dried flower. She laid them out on her living room floor. Five boxes. Five items. A locket, a flower, a faded letter, a child's drawing, a music box. Each one old, used, forgotten.

Lena stared at them. A knot formed in her stomach. These items felt... familiar. Not in a clear way, but like a faint echo. She picked up the music box. It was heavy, dark wood. She turned the key. A tiny, tinny tune played. It was a simple, sweet lullaby. A sharp pain shot through her head. A picture flashed in her mind: a small girl, crying, holding a wooden box just like this. The image was gone before she could grab it.

The next day, the final package arrived. It was smaller than the others. Lena opened it with shaking hands. Inside was a small, hand-mirror. It had a wooden frame, and it was old, just like the other things. But this mirror was special. On the back, etched into the wood, were words: *Remember, Keeper. The truth is inside.*

Lena held the mirror. She looked at her reflection. Her face was pale, scared. She turned the mirror over. She traced the words with her finger. "Remember, Keeper. The truth is inside." Her breath hitched. *Inside?* Inside what? Inside the mirror? Inside her?

She looked at her reflection again. This time, she saw something different. Not just her own fearful face, but something behind her eyes. A flicker of something lost.

### Conclusion
Suddenly, she understood. The packages were not from someone else. They were from her. Not the Lena who woke up each morning, but a different Lena, one who had hidden away a terrible memory, one who had called herself 'The Keeper' of that secret. The music box tune, the child's drawing, the locket, the faded letter, the dried flower – they were all clues, breadcrumbs she had left for herself. Each item was a key to a locked part of her mind, a part that held a truth she couldn't face, but now had to. The final package, the mirror, showed her the sender. She was the sender. She was 'The Keeper'. And the truth, she now knew, was buried deep within her own forgotten past, waiting to be unlocked.
