# The Midnight Delivery

**Genre:** Thriller | **Date:** 2026-10-30

### Characters
*   Arthur: A careful, slightly anxious delivery driver.
*   Sarah: Arthur's wife, a loving prankster.
*   Mike: Arthur's best friend, also in on the prank.

### The Story
The phone buzzed. Arthur looked at the screen. A number he didn't know. He was just finishing his shift, tired from a long day of moving boxes. "Hello?"
"Arthur. We have a special job for you." The voice was flat, deep, and spoke with careful pauses. "High pay. Secret package. Midnight delivery. Do not ask questions. Do not be late. Do not tell anyone."
Arthur usually said no to odd jobs, but the money mentioned made him pause. He had bills to pay. "Where's the pick-up?" he asked, a knot forming in his stomach.

He drove to a dark alley behind an old bank. A figure in a hooded jacket waited beside a van. The person said nothing, just pointed to a large, unmarked box on the ground. It was heavy, wrapped tight in plain brown paper and duct tape. Arthur heard a faint hum coming from inside, like a quiet, steady buzz. He lifted it. It felt awkward, oddly shaped. "Where does it go?" he asked. The figure just handed him a small, cheap mobile phone. "Details will come to this. Drive."

Arthur got back in his own van. He put the box carefully on the passenger seat. The hum was still there, a soft, steady thrum. He looked at the burner phone. A text popped up: "Warehouse 7. Old industrial district. Midnight. We are watching. Do not deviate."
His heart hammered. "We are watching." He checked his mirrors. Was that a dark sedan two cars back, following his turns? He sped up a little. The sedan matched his speed.

He drove through quiet streets, the hum from the box a low, constant worry. He felt eyes on him. The sedan kept its distance, but it was there. He took a sharp turn, then another, trying to lose it. No luck. He got another text: "You are late. Pick up the pace. No detours."
Sweat trickled down his back. This wasn't just a delivery; it was something else. A drug drop? A bomb? He imagined flashing lights, sirens, jail. Or worse. He gripped the steering wheel, knuckles white. The hum in the box seemed to get louder, or maybe it was just his nerves.

Finally, he reached the old industrial district. It was completely dark, silent, except for the wind whistling through broken windows. Warehouse 7 stood like a tomb. He pulled up, engine idling. The sedan was gone. He looked around nervously. No one. Just shadows.
The burner phone vibrated. "Place the package on the loading dock. Then leave. Do not look back."
Arthur swallowed hard. He grabbed the heavy box. It felt like it weighed a ton. He walked towards the loading dock, each step heavy. He placed the package down, gently. The humming stopped. Dead silence. He turned to run.

### Conclusion
"Stop, Arthur!"
Lights suddenly flooded the entire area. Arthur spun around, blinking in the harsh glare. People emerged from behind old crates and rusted machinery.
"Surprise!"
His wife, Sarah, was there, beaming, holding a banner that read, 'Happy 40th, Arthur!' His best friend, Mike, stood beside her, grinning. Other friends and family cheered.
Arthur stared, bewildered. "What... what is this?"
Sarah walked up, laughing. "Happy birthday, darling! You look terrified! It was Mike's idea for the 'midnight delivery' thriller theme."
Mike stepped forward, patting Arthur's shoulder. "We know how much you love those spy movies, mate. And you're always so observant, we knew you'd notice the 'tail'." He pointed to the sedan parked a little distance away. "That was me. Sarah sent the texts from a spare phone. The 'humming' in the box? That's your present."
Arthur looked at the box, then back at his smiling wife. "My present?"
Sarah nodded. "An extra-large, custom-made Rube Goldberg machine! It's delicate, that's why we had to be so careful. We knew you'd never open a 'secret package'." She hugged him tight. "You were late, though. All those detours."
Arthur could only shake his head, a huge, relieved laugh bubbling out. "You absolute devils! I thought I was delivering a nuclear warhead!" He looked at the box, no longer menacing, but an exciting gift. The tangy flavor of fear was gone, replaced by the sweet taste of a truly unforgettable birthday surprise.
