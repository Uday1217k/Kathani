# The Jam Jar

**Genre:** Horror | **Date:** 2026-09-01

### Characters
*   Linda: A woman who lives alone, loves her routines.

### The Story
Linda loved her mornings. She worked from home, so she could take her time. Every day, her breakfast was the same: two slices of toast and a big spoon of raspberry jam. It was her little comfort.

One Wednesday, she finished her toast. She put the lid back on the jam jar. She noticed the jam was halfway down the jar. She knew she'd used a lot, so that made sense. She put the jar back in the fridge.

Thursday morning, Linda took out the jam. She opened it. She stopped. The jam was not halfway down. It was almost full again. She stared. Had she been wrong? Maybe she hadn't used as much as she thought yesterday. She shrugged. People make mistakes. She scooped out a generous dollop for her toast. It tasted sweet and familiar.

Friday morning, the same thing happened. She remembered clearly using a large spoon. She even thought, "This jar will be empty soon." But there it was, almost full again. Linda felt a little shiver. This wasn't right. She lived alone. She locked her doors. Nobody could be playing a trick. She decided to test it.

She took a spoon and dug deep into the jam. She ate a lot of it. Then she carefully marked the jam line with a marker pen on the outside of the jar. "There," she mumbled. "Now we'll see." She put it in the fridge.

Saturday morning, Linda hurried to the fridge. She pulled out the jam jar. Her jaw dropped. The jam was higher than the line she'd drawn. It was almost at the very top. It looked like a fresh, unopened jar. She touched the marker line. It was definitely below the jam.

A strange feeling came over her. Not fear exactly, more like a deep wrongness. The jam jar felt heavy in her hand. She stared at the red, glistening surface. It looked like normal jam, smelled like normal jam. But it wasn't. It was doing something impossible.

She put the jar back. She didn't eat any toast that morning. Or the next. She just watched the jam. Every morning, it was full, sometimes bulging slightly under the lid, like it was pushing up. It felt like it was growing, somehow. It felt like it was watching *her*.

She didn't tell anyone. Who would believe her? She started to get very worried. Her stomach felt jumpy all the time. She couldn't stop looking at the jam. It was just a jar, just jam. But it was wrong. So wrong.

Monday morning. Linda walked to the fridge. She opened the door slowly. The jam jar was there. It was completely full, pressed against the lid, the glass looking strained. A tiny, almost silent "gloop" sound came from inside.

Linda picked up the jar. It felt warm in her hand. She peered at the jam. It was thick, deep red. And then she saw it.

### Conclusion
Right in the middle of the raspberry jam, staring up at her, was a small, perfectly formed human eye. It was deep blue, like her own, and it blinked. Once. Slow and wet. Then it closed, and the jam surface settled, hiding it again. Linda dropped the jar. It hit the floor with a wet thud, shattering glass and splattering red jam everywhere. But she didn't care about the mess. She just stared at the broken jar, at the pulsing, red goo. She could almost taste the sweetness, the fruit. And now, all she could think was: how many times had she eaten that eye? How many times had it watched her from inside the jar? The sweet taste now turned sour and hot in her mouth, like a burning, hidden thing.
