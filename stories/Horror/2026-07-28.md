# The Listening Companion

**Genre:** Horror | **Date:** 2026-07-28

### Characters
*   Martha: An older woman, lives alone, a bit lonely.
*   Daisy: A porcelain doll, Martha's new companion.

### The Story
Martha lived in a small house. Her days were quiet. Too quiet. She often talked to herself, just to hear a voice.

For her birthday, her niece sent her a gift. It was a doll, made of smooth porcelain. It had big blue eyes and painted red lips that held a small, fixed smile. Her niece wrote: "For company, Aunt Martha. She'll always listen." Martha named her Daisy.

"Hello, Daisy," Martha said that evening. "It's just us now." She put the doll on her armchair, facing her. Daisy sat there, silent, watching.

Martha started telling Daisy everything. About the rude mailman. About her stiff knees. About the pigeon that always landed on her window sill at dawn. Daisy never moved. Never blinked. She just listened with those wide blue eyes.

One morning, Martha woke up. The pigeon wasn't at her window. It wasn't there the next day either. And the mailman? A new, young man delivered the mail now. He smiled. "Well, that's nice," Martha mumbled to Daisy.

Then came the bigger things. Martha had a neighbour, Mr. Henderson, who played loud music late at night. "Oh, Daisy," Martha sighed one evening, "I wish he'd just move far away." A week later, a "For Sale" sign popped up in Mr. Henderson's yard. Two weeks after that, he was gone. His house was empty.

A cold feeling started in Martha's stomach. "It's just a coincidence," she told herself. But the feeling stayed.

Martha had a recurring pain in her shoulder. She told Daisy, "I hate doctors. I wish I didn't have to go for that check-up next month." The day before her appointment, the clinic called. There was a problem. Her appointment was cancelled, indefinitely.

Martha stared at Daisy. The doll sat perfectly still, porcelain face unmoving. Its eyes seemed to know. Martha tried to stop talking to it. She tried to ignore it. But the house felt even quieter then, filled with Daisy's silent waiting.

One afternoon, Martha decided. This was too much. She picked up Daisy. The porcelain felt cold against her hands. She marched to the trash can outside and dropped the doll inside. "Good riddance," she whispered.

She went back inside, heart thumping. The house felt lighter. She smiled, pouring herself a cup of tea.

Suddenly, she heard a soft thud. It came from the living room. Martha walked slowly, carefully. There, sitting in the armchair, was Daisy. Its blue eyes were fixed on the empty spot where Martha had just been sitting.

Panic bloomed in Martha's chest. She grabbed the doll again, ran to her car, and drove far, far away. She left Daisy on a park bench. She didn't look back.

Days passed. Martha felt a strange peace. Until she opened her front door.

### Conclusion
Daisy sat on the floor, right inside the door. Its small, fixed smile seemed a little wider. Its blue eyes seemed to twinkle. Martha screamed. The doll didn't move. It just sat there, waiting. Waiting to listen. Because Daisy didn't just listen to words anymore. Daisy listened to Martha's deepest, quietest thoughts. And Daisy loved to help. Martha closed the door and locked it. From the inside. She knew she would never leave again. And Daisy would always be there, her perfect, silent companion, listening, knowing, and making sure Martha was safe... from everything, and everyone, but Daisy.
