# The Surface of Things

**Genre:** Horror | **Date:** 2026-11-17

### Characters
Jim: A man in his late 30s, lives alone, works a regular office job. He is quiet and keeps to himself.

### The Story
Jim woke up like he always did, at six-thirty on the dot. He padded to the bathroom, splashing cold water on his face. When he looked in the mirror, his reflection seemed fine. But then, it blinked a tiny fraction of a second *after* Jim did. Jim stared. He blinked again, slowly. The reflection blinked with him, but again, a hair too late. Jim shook his head. He must be tired.

The next morning, it was more noticeable. Jim frowned at his tired eyes. His reflection didn't just frown back; a quick, almost invisible smile flickered across its face before it copied Jim’s frown. Jim gasped, leaning closer. The reflection’s smile was gone, replaced by his own worried face. He told himself it was just a trick of the light, or his tired mind playing games.

Days turned into a week. The reflection started to do more little things. Jim would scratch his nose, and the reflection would seem to smooth its hair first. Jim would open his mouth to speak, and the reflection’s lips would move, a soundless word, before Jim made a peep. What unsettled Jim the most was how the reflection looked. While Jim felt drained from his long workdays, with dark circles under his eyes, his reflection grew brighter. Its skin looked fresh, its eyes sparkled. It seemed full of energy, always with a hint of a knowing smile that Jim couldn't quite place on his own face.

One Tuesday, Jim stood before the bathroom mirror, ready for work. He was wearing his blue striped shirt. But in the mirror, his reflection wore a plain white one. Jim stared, his heart thumping. He turned around, then back to the mirror. His shirt was still blue. The reflection’s was still white. Jim reached out a trembling hand. The reflection reached out too, its hand looking stronger, healthier than Jim's own. For a split second, the reflection’s eyes held a strange pity. Then, it blinked, and the plain white shirt was gone. His own blue striped shirt was back, perfectly matched.

Fear started to claw at Jim. He covered the bathroom mirror with a towel. He avoided the glass doors at the office. He even turned his phone screen face down when it wasn't in use, afraid of catching a glimpse of the vibrant, happy stranger that looked just like him. But reflections were everywhere. A shop window, a polished kettle, a puddle on the pavement. Each time, he saw it. The reflection, always a step ahead, always looking better, happier. Always watching him with that unnerving, quiet knowledge.

One evening, he couldn't take it anymore. He stood in front of the towel-covered mirror, his hands shaking. He ripped the towel off. There it was. His reflection. It was smiling, a wide, cheerful grin. Jim felt a deep, chilling emptiness spread through his chest. He looked so tired, so worn out. But the reflection… it looked like Jim used to look, years ago, before the weight of the world had settled on him. It looked full of life, of hope. Suddenly, the reflection’s eyes welled up. Tears streamed down its perfect, healthy face. It started to mouth words, desperate, silent pleas. Jim couldn't hear them, but he understood the agony in its eyes. He felt a dull ache behind his own eyes, a phantom pain that wasn't quite his. He tried to move his lips, to speak, to ask what was happening. But his jaw felt heavy, unresponsive.

### Conclusion
He watched his reflection smile back, a wide, confident smile. Then, *he* smiled too, his own lips curving without his command. The reflection raised a hand, and his own hand followed, rising in perfect sync. The eyes in the glass were bright, full of life. *His* eyes, inside, felt dull, fading. He tried to scream, but no sound came. Only the reflection’s happy hum, a tune he almost recognized, escaped *his* throat. He was trapped, a silent scream behind glass. The world outside the mirror, his world, was now its own.
