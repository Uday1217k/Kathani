# The Other Self

**Genre:** Horror | **Date:** 2026-11-03

### Characters
Arthur (a man who values order and his perfect appearance)

### The Story
Arthur liked things just right. His shirts were always pressed. His shoes shone. Every morning, he stood in front of his bathroom mirror. He would check his hair, his tie, the straight line of his collar. This daily routine was important. It set his day.

One Tuesday, Arthur looked at himself. He saw a wrinkle on his reflection's shirt. Not a big one, but it was there, just below the collar. Arthur frowned. His own shirt felt perfectly smooth. He tugged at his shirt. The wrinkle in the mirror stayed. He leaned closer. It vanished. Arthur shook his head. It must be the light.

The next day, it happened again. This time, his reflection's hair had a strand out of place. A small, messy bit above the ear. Arthur’s own hair was neatly combed, as always. He raised a hand to smooth his hair. The reflection's hand copied him, but the stray strand stayed put. Arthur rubbed his eyes. He was tired, maybe. He had not slept well. He blamed that.

By Thursday, Arthur felt a knot in his stomach. He looked in the mirror. His reflection blinked, but a moment *before* Arthur did. Then, as Arthur adjusted his watch, the reflection’s mouth twitched into a small, sad smile. Arthur wasn't smiling. He was focused on his watch strap. The smile in the glass faded as quickly as it came. Arthur felt cold. This was not right.

Friday morning. Arthur walked into the bathroom slowly. He paused before the mirror. He took a deep breath. He looked up.
His reflection stood there, looking back. The reflection’s shoulders were slumped. Its eyes had dark circles. Its tie was loose. It looked utterly tired, worn down. Arthur felt none of this. He was ready for work, focused, sharp. He stared at the tired face in the glass.

"No," Arthur whispered. "You are not me."
The reflection opened its mouth. It did not speak. Instead, a long, weary sigh came from its lungs. Arthur felt the air leave his own chest. It was a sigh he knew well, but had never allowed himself to make. It was the sound of a man who had been holding his breath for a very long time.

### Conclusion
The reflection slowly raised a hand, not to copy Arthur, but to touch its own tired face. Its eyes, deep and heavy, met Arthur’s. And in them, Arthur saw not a stranger, but a silent scream. A scream from the part of himself he had locked away, hidden behind pressed shirts and perfect smiles. The reflection was not trying to take his place. It was just begging to be seen. And Arthur, standing rigid and straight, felt his perfect world crack. The mirror had not created a monster; it had simply shown him the one he already was, tired and alone, trapped behind a facade he himself had built. The other Arthur, the real Arthur, was finally looking back.
