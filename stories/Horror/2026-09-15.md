# The Deep Seed

**Genre:** Horror | **Date:** 2026-09-15

### Characters
John: A quiet, older man who lives alone.

### The Story
John was a man who liked things plain. His house was plain, his food was plain, and his days rolled out simple and quiet. One hot afternoon, he was fixing his old shed. The roof needed new wood. He dug a small hole in the dirt beside the shed to steady a new post. His shovel hit something hard. Not a rock. It made a dull, thudding sound.

He knelt and dug around it with his hands. It was a stone, dark as night, smooth as glass. It fit perfectly in his palm, shaped like a fat egg, but flatter on one side. He had never seen anything like it. It was terribly cold, even in the summer sun, a deep chill that went straight through his skin. He felt a strange pull to it, a need to keep it close. He brought it inside and placed it on his kitchen counter, next to the fruit bowl.

Days passed. Small, odd things began to happen. John’s morning tea tasted metallic, like old pennies. His kitchen clock would stop for hours, then jump forward. His cat, Mittens, a fluffy old ginger, usually rubbed against his legs and purred loud enough to shake the floorboards. Now, she kept her distance, her green eyes fixed on the stone on the counter, her tail tucked low. John tried to blame it on old age, on his mind playing tricks.

He put the stone in a drawer in his bedroom, out of sight. The next morning, he woke up, and there it was, sitting on his bedside table. He blinked, rubbing his eyes. How could it have moved? He started to feel a strange coldness in his bones, not the kind that meant he was sick, but a deep, heavy chill that made him shiver even in warm rooms. He slept badly, filled with vivid dreams of being buried, wrapped tight in wet earth.

He knew he had to get rid of it. He wrapped the stone in an old cloth and walked down to the river, the cold weight heavy in his pocket. He stood by the rushing water, pulled out the stone, and held it over the dark current. He tried to drop it. His fingers locked. His hand wouldn't open. A cold dread spread from his palm up his arm. His mind screamed, "Let go!" but his body would not obey. He brought the stone home, shaking. He tried to throw it in the big rubbish bin outside, the one that went to the dump. As he walked back into the house, he felt it again, the familiar, icy weight in his pocket.

John looked at himself in the mirror. His face looked thinner, his eyes a bit hollow. His skin felt dry, like old parchment. He touched his cheek. It was cold. He stared at the stone, sitting on his open palm. It seemed to pulse, a slow, soft beat he felt deep in his own gut.

### Conclusion
Then he saw it. A tiny crack, a hair-thin line, ran along its perfect, dark surface. And from the crack, a sound, like dry sand shifting, just in his ear: *Warm…*
