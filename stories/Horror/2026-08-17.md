# The Dog's Gaze

**Genre:** Horror | **Date:** 2026-08-17

### Characters
Sarah: A young woman living alone.
Max: Sarah's old, loyal Labrador dog.

### The Story
Sarah loved Max more than anything. He was a big, yellow Labrador, a little grey around the muzzle now, but still full of love. Every evening, he'd greet her at the door with a wagging tail and a slobbery lick. They had a good routine: walks, dinner, then TV with Max curled up by her feet. Life was simple and good.

Then Max started acting odd. It began with little things. He’d stare at an empty corner of the living room, a soft whine rumbling in his chest. Sarah would check, thinking maybe a mouse or a spider, but there was never anything there. "Just getting old, boy," she'd tell him, stroking his head. Max would blink slowly, then go back to his staring.

A few weeks later, it got worse. Max stopped sleeping by her feet. He’d choose the armchair across the room, watching her with a strange, fixed gaze. Sometimes, when she walked past a mirror, he’d let out a low growl, not at her, but at her reflection. Sarah found it upsetting. Her sweet, gentle dog was changing. She took him to the vet. Dr. Miller checked Max over thoroughly. "He's healthy as a horse, Sarah," he said with a shrug. "Maybe a touch of doggy dementia? It happens."

Sarah went home feeling worse. Max wasn't sick. He was just... scared. And he seemed to be scared of *her*. He'd flinch if she reached out too fast. He'd bark if she laughed suddenly. One morning, she saw him cowering under the kitchen table, watching her with wide, terrified eyes as she made coffee. It broke her heart.

One particularly dark evening, a storm raged outside. Rain lashed against the windows. Sarah was sitting on the couch, reading a book, trying to ignore the nervous whimpers coming from Max. He was pressed into the furthest corner of the room, his body trembling. Suddenly, he let out a sharp, frantic bark. Then another. He wasn't barking at the storm. He was barking at Sarah. He lowered his head, a guttural growl escaping him, his teeth bared. His eyes, usually so warm and full of love, were wide with pure terror.

Sarah felt a chill that had nothing to do with the stormy weather. "Max, stop it," she said, her voice shaking a little. "What's wrong?" She stood up slowly, trying to approach him. Max let out a desperate, whimpering howl and flattened himself against the wall, trying to press himself *through* it, away from her. He was absolutely terrified. His gaze was fixed, not on her face, but somewhere around her shoulder. She turned, quickly, seeing nothing. Just the ordinary living room.

### Conclusion
Then she looked back at Max. His eyes were still wide, still fixed on her. But this time, she understood. He wasn't scared of a ghost behind her, or a shadow on the wall. He was scared of *her*. And as the thought settled, a deep, unsettling coldness bloomed just under her skin, spreading from her chest. It was a coldness she had never felt before, not from the stormy air, but from *within*. And for the first time, she felt her own fear begin to bloom.
