# The Smallest Stone

**Genre:** Horror | **Date:** 2026-11-10

### Characters
Martha: A woman who lives alone, careful and tidy.
Sarah: Martha's sister, sensible and a little dismissive.
Dr. Evans: Martha's family doctor, kind but practical.

### The Story
Martha liked her quiet evenings. After work, she'd cook a simple meal. Tonight, it was tomato soup with a warm roll. She dipped the bread, took a bite. A strange, tiny *crunch*. Not a seed, not a spice. Like a pebble, but smooth. She chewed carefully. Nothing. She swallowed, feeling a little odd, and pushed the thought away.

The next day, it happened again. In her scrambled eggs. A small, hard thing. She picked it out. It was smaller than a grain of rice, roundish, and perfectly smooth. Like a tiny, polished bead. She looked at it closely, then flicked it into the bin. "Just bad luck," she thought, a shiver running through her.

It wasn't bad luck. It was every meal. In her salad, her pasta, even her morning coffee – she felt the faint scrape against her teeth. She started checking her food, spreading it out. She bought new food, fresh from the market. Cooked everything from scratch in spotless pans. Still, the tiny, hard things were there. Always one. Sometimes two. She started to dread eating.

She called her sister, Sarah. "There's something in my food, Sarah. Tiny little stones." Sarah listened, then said, "Martha, are you sure? Maybe it's just a bit of grit from the lettuce? Wash it better." Martha felt a rush of anger. "I'm washing everything! It's like they're just... appearing." Sarah just hummed, a sound that meant she didn't believe a word.

Martha went to Dr. Evans. She brought a tiny plastic bag with three of the 'stones' she'd managed to save. Dr. Evans held them up to the light. "Hmm, they look like calcium deposits," he said. "Are you getting enough minerals?" He checked her teeth, her gums. "Everything looks fine, Martha. No loose fillings. Maybe it's just your imagination playing tricks."

Imagination? She felt them. She tasted them. Martha stopped eating proper meals. She only had liquids – smoothies, broth. But even then, sometimes she'd feel it, a tiny bump sliding down her throat. She grew thin, her eyes looked tired. She was starving, but the fear of the tiny stones was worse than hunger.

One night, she sat with a glass of water. Her stomach rumbled. She took a tiny sip. Nothing. Another sip. She felt a strange itch deep in her gum, behind her back teeth. She rubbed it with her tongue. A tiny prick. She pressed harder. A sharp *pop*, and then something small and smooth landed on her tongue. It was one of *them*.

### Conclusion
Her heart hammered. She hadn't been eating anything. She hadn't even opened her mouth to speak since she sat down. She looked in the mirror, pulling her lip back, peering at the spot. A tiny, raw crater where the 'stone' had been. The 'stones' weren't in her food. They were growing inside her, slowly, painfully, then breaking off. Her own body was making them, shedding them into her mouth. Every meal, she had been finding pieces of herself. And there were still so many more to come.
