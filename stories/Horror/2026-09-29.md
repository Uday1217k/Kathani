# The A-Line

**Genre:** Horror | **Date:** 2026-09-29

### Characters
Arthur (a man who lives alone, very neat and orderly)

### The Story
Arthur liked things just so. His small apartment was always clean, every book on the shelf lined up, every spice jar alphabetized. He lived alone, and he liked it that way. No one to mess up his perfect lines. One sunny afternoon, he bought four new teacups. They were simple, plain white ceramic, without any patterns. He liked them because they were neat. He placed them carefully in his kitchen cabinet, in a perfectly straight line, each cup an equal distance from the next.

The next morning, Arthur opened the cabinet. The teacups were not in a straight line. One was a little bit out, angled oddly. He sighed, figuring he must have nudged it himself without noticing. He fixed them, making sure they were truly perfect this time. He even took a quick photo on his phone, just to prove he had done it right.

But the next day, they were wrong again. This time, two cups were pushed together, and the other two were far apart. Arthur felt a small prickle of unease. He knew he hadn't done that. He lived alone. There was no one else. He straightened them once more, carefully, slowly. He even drew a faint pencil line on the shelf to guide him.

That night, before bed, Arthur went back to the kitchen. The cups were exactly as he had left them. He smiled, feeling silly for being so worried. He went to sleep. The next morning, his smile vanished. The cups were not on the pencil line. They were arranged in a tight square, almost touching. Arthur's heart started to beat a little faster. This was impossible.

He took the cups out of the cabinet. He put them in a cardboard box and pushed the box deep under his bed. "There," he said aloud to the empty room. "Fixed." He felt a bit foolish talking to cups, but also a bit safer.

When he woke up, he walked into the kitchen. On the counter, standing in a perfect triangle, were the four white cups. They had moved from under his bed. Arthur felt a cold rush through him. He sat down hard in a chair.

He borrowed an old webcam from his friend. He set it up in the kitchen, pointing at the counter. He started recording as soon as it got dark. Then he went to bed, a blanket pulled tight around his ears.

The next morning, he quickly made coffee and played the video. He watched, holding his breath. For hours, nothing happened. Then, just after midnight, one cup shifted. Slowly, silently, it slid across the counter. Then another. And another. The cups moved without being touched. They moved until they formed a shape Arthur recognized. It was a capital letter 'A', but crooked, almost like a child had drawn it.

Arthur watched the video again and again. The cups always formed an 'A'. Sometimes it was a bit messy, sometimes clearer. He tried arranging them differently before bed, but they always went back to the 'A'. He tried talking to them, even yelling, but they just sat there, plain and white.

One night, he couldn't sleep. He watched the live camera feed on his computer. He saw the cups move, slowly forming the 'A' again. He watched it finish, feeling a strange mix of fear and something else he couldn't name. Then, as he watched, the cups didn't stop. They kept moving. Slower this time. More careful. The 'A' melted away, and new shapes began to form. They looked like numbers. Days, months. A date.

### Conclusion
Arthur felt a chill deeper than ever before. He watched the last cup slide into place. The date was clear: October 18th. It was three weeks away. He suddenly understood. The 'A' wasn't for Arthur. It was for the *next* person. The person who would own the apartment after him. And the cups weren't just moving; they were telling him exactly when he would be gone.
