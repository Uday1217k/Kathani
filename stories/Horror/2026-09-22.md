# The Button-Gatherer

**Genre:** Horror | **Date:** 2026-09-22

### Characters
*   Clara: An organized woman living alone in an apartment.

### The Story
Clara liked things neat. Her apartment was small but always in order. A place for everything, and everything in its place. That’s why the first missing button bothered her so much. It was from her good coat, a deep green one she loved. She’d put the coat on the hanger the night before, buttoned up. The next morning, one button, the third from the top, was just gone. Not fallen off, not loose. Just *gone*.

She searched everywhere. Under the sofa, behind the curtains, even inside her shoes. Nothing. She sighed, put it down to her getting older and losing her touch. She’d sew on a new one.

But then other things started to vanish. A tiny, silver earring, one of a pair. A specific thimble, engraved with a flower. Her small, sharp nail file. These weren't important things, just bits and pieces of her daily life. But they weren't turning up in strange places after she used them. They were simply... gone.

Then, they started to reappear. The earring was found tucked inside a sugar jar. The thimble was under her pillow. The nail file lay neatly on her doorstep one morning, just outside the front door. This was not forgetfulness. Someone was moving her things.

Clara felt a cold knot in her stomach. She lived alone. The doors were always locked. The windows too. She checked them twice a day now. She started leaving little tests: a hair across her wardrobe door, a sprinkle of dust on the windowsill. Every morning, the hair was undisturbed. The dust remained. No sign of anyone. Yet, her things still shifted. A teacup from the cupboard found its way to the bathroom sink. Her reading glasses would be on the kitchen counter instead of her bedside table.

She bought a small, cheap camera that sensed movement. She put it in her living room, facing the main space. She watched the footage every night. Hours of empty room. Until one Tuesday. At around 3 AM, a flicker. A shadow moving very fast, right at the edge of the frame, near the baseboard. It was too quick to make out. Just a smear. The next night, another flicker. A faint clicking sound seemed to come from the camera's tiny speaker, barely there.

Clara couldn't sleep. The apartment felt different. Not empty, but full of eyes she couldn't see. She started putting out a specific item each night. A shiny, bright red button she’d kept in her sewing box for years. It was small, no bigger than her pinky nail, and very noticeable. She placed it on her nightstand, right in the middle, easy to spot.

The next morning, the red button was gone.

Clara didn't panic this time. She just felt a strange calm. She knew it would happen. She sat on her bed, quiet, listening. The apartment was still. Then, from the wall directly opposite her bed, near the floor, a faint sound began. It was a delicate, almost musical *tinkling*. Like tiny bells, or very small, carefully handled glass beads. It lasted only a few seconds, then stopped.

Clara got off the bed. She knelt down, her ear to the wall. Nothing. She reached for her phone, turned on the flashlight. She scanned the baseboard. And there, almost hidden by the carpet's edge, was a tiny crack. It was thinner than a pencil, barely noticeable.

She brought the phone's light closer, pressing her eye to the crack. The light pushed into the darkness. At first, she saw only dust and wood. Then, as her eyes adjusted, a tunnel. A tiny, perfectly shaped tunnel, leading into a space that shimmered faintly. It was like looking into a dollhouse, but it wasn't made of plastic. It was real.

Tiny furniture, fashioned from lint and wood splinters, stood in miniature rooms. Cobweb threads formed delicate drapes. And there, sitting on a tiny, acorn-cap stool, was a figure. It was small, no taller than her thumb, but clearly humanoid. It had thin limbs, large, dark eyes, and wore clothes made from fabric scraps. In its tiny hands, it held something.

The red button.

The figure was carefully polishing it with a scrap of silk. As Clara watched, frozen, the tiny creature slowly lifted its head. Its large, dark eyes met hers through the crack. A moment passed, silent, unmoving. Then, a tiny, slender hand slowly lifted from the button. And the creature, with a delicate, curious grace, waved.

### Conclusion
Clara didn't scream. She didn't move away. She just stared at the tiny waving hand, at the miniature world behind the crack in her wall, and knew she would never again feel truly alone in her neat, tidy apartment. Or truly in charge.
