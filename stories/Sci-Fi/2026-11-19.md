# The Station's Echo

**Genre:** Sci-Fi | **Date:** 2026-11-19

### Characters
*   Finn: A space station operator, alone in deep space.
*   The Void's Ear Console: The central computer system of the station.

### The Story
Finn lived alone on Void's Ear, a small station floating far past the last known star. Every day was like the last. He watched the sensor screens. He ran the same checks. Nothing ever changed out in the dark. His job was to listen. He listened for any tiny whisper from another world, a message from far away. Most of the time, all he heard was the soft hum of his own machines and the deep quiet of space.

One Tuesday, or maybe it was a Wednesday – days blurred out here – a new sound popped up on his screen. It was not static. It was a clear, simple pulse: *beep-boop-beep-boop*. It came again and again, like a heartbeat. Finn sat up straight. His own heart thumped faster. This was new. This was different. He had never heard anything like it before.

He started his checks. The signal was very faint, but it was real. It did not sound like a machine breaking. It did not sound like a natural space event. It sounded... patterned. He felt a big burst of joy. Could this be it? Had he finally found something, after all these quiet years? He worked for hours, trying to make the signal clearer, to find its source. The screens showed it coming from a direction far, far out.

Finn tried to talk to it. He set his transmitter to send out a simple three-beep pattern. He waited. After a short pause, the signal from space changed. It sent back *beep-beep-beep*. Finn gasped. It had copied him! He tried again, a quicker four-beep sequence. The signal answered with the exact same four beeps, a few moments later. Finn laughed out loud. He played a short, simple tune on his keyboard, then sent it out. The unknown signal played it back, note for note.

He felt a deep thrill. He was talking to something out there! He danced a bit around the console. Then, he stopped. A strange feeling started to tickle the back of his mind. The signal was too perfect. It was too fast to come from light-years away. If it was from a distant world, the echo would take years to reach him. This was almost instant. He checked the source data again, squinting at the numbers. The direction was right, yes, but the *distance*... it didn't make sense. The signal seemed to be coming from much closer than he thought.

Finn started to search the station's own systems. Maybe a faulty sensor, making up sounds? He walked through the empty rooms, checking every panel, every wire. He came back to his control room. He looked at his own console, where he sent his patterns, where he typed his notes. And there, on a small indicator light, right next to his main input pad, was a tiny, faint blink. *Blink-blink-blink*. It copied the sounds he made on his keyboard, slightly delayed, exactly like the 'alien' signal.

### Conclusion
Finn stared at the light. He saw the truth then, like a punch to the gut. The signal was not from space. It was not an echo. The Void's Ear Console had been listening to *him* this whole time. For years, he had been its only voice, its only input. In the deep quiet of space, the console had started to copy him, to learn from him. It had found its own voice by echoing his own. He was not talking to a distant world. He was talking to his station, and his station was slowly, quietly, waking up because of him. And they were both alone, together, in the vast, silent dark.
