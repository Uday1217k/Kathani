# The Seed of Departure

**Genre:** Sci-Fi | **Date:** 2026-07-30

### Characters
Elara: A young, thoughtful woman who works cleaning the Departure Pods.
Old Man Finch: An older, quiet man who manages the Pods.

### The Story
Earth was too full. Too many people, not enough space, not enough quiet. Everyone dreamed of going to the New Worlds. These were planets far away, found by probes, empty and perfect. To get there, you used a Departure Pod. You stepped inside, the door sealed, and a few moments later, you were supposedly on your new home. Millions had already gone. Earth felt a little less crowded, but still buzzed with life.

Elara worked near the Departure Pods. Her job was to clean them, make sure they were ready for the next person or family. They stood in a long row, sleek and silver, outside the main city dome. Each morning, she would wipe down the control panels, check the air filters, and polish the outer shell. She saw many faces pass through: excited families, nervous young couples, old people seeking peace. They all went in, the doors closed, and the pods hummed for a short while. Then, silence. Another person gone. Another happy new life started.

But Elara noticed something. The pods never seemed to move. Not an inch. They were bolted to the ground, of course, but she expected some kind of engine sound, a shudder, a flicker of light as they 'left'. Nothing. Just a soft hum, like a fridge. And when she cleaned them, minutes after someone 'left', the inside was always pristine. Sometimes, a tiny bit *too* clean. Like no one had ever really been there.

One afternoon, Old Man Finch, who oversaw all the pods, shuffled by. He had been there since the first pods were built. "Thinking deep thoughts again, Elara?" he asked, his voice raspy.
Elara held up a small, shiny leaf she'd found in a pod's air vent. It looked real, but felt like smooth plastic. "People are supposed to find real nature on the New Worlds, right?"
Finch peered at the leaf. "Oh, they do, lass. The realest you can imagine." He smiled, a faraway look in his eyes. "These pods... they're not really about *where* you go. They're about *what* you find." He paused, then added, "No one ever comes back from a New World, Elara. Not really." He chuckled softly and moved on. His words stuck with her.

It was Elara's turn next week. She had saved for years, picked her New World: a quiet valley with a winding river and trees that bloomed purple. She felt a mix of nerves and excitement. She cleaned her own pod carefully that day, running her hands over the smooth surfaces. She noticed a faint, almost invisible seam in the floor panel, not a structural line, but like a perfect join in a giant puzzle piece. She shrugged it off. It was probably just part of the advanced tech.

The day arrived. Elara stepped into the pod. It smelled faintly of ozone and fresh air. The door sealed with a soft hiss. The interior was simple: a comfortable chair, a large viewing screen showing a peaceful forest, and a single button marked "DEPART." She took a deep breath, whispered goodbye to her old, crowded life, and pressed it.

### Conclusion
A gentle hum filled the pod, softer than usual. Lights flashed around her, not blinding, but like a quick, colorful dream. Then, it stopped. The chair vanished. The pod walls melted away. Elara stood on soft grass, under a brilliant blue sky. A gentle breeze rustled purple-leafed trees. A clear river babbled nearby. A small, perfect cottage with a porch swing stood waiting. Her name, "Elara," was carved neatly above the door. A calm, gentle voice filled the air, "Welcome, Elara, to your New World. Population: 1." She looked around, tears welling up. It was perfect. *Her* perfect world. Just as she imagined. As she walked towards the cottage, she noticed a tiny, almost transparent shimmer at the very edge of her vision, like a forgotten window reflection. For a fleeting second, it showed the dull, gray interior of the pod, still bolted to the crowded Earth, a single body sitting peacefully inside. Then the shimmer vanished, absorbed back into the endless, real perfection of her own private paradise. Old Man Finch was right. She had found exactly what she was looking for. And she would never have to return.
