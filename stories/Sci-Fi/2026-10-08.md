# The Empathy Stone

**Genre:** Sci-Fi | **Date:** 2026-10-08

### Characters
Elara: An antique shop owner. Curious, a bit quiet.
The Empathy Stone: A smooth, dull grey stone.

### The Story
Elara ran an antique shop. It was dusty and smelled of old wood and forgotten memories. Most days were quiet. One Tuesday, a new box of old junk came in. Inside, wrapped in yellowed newspaper, Elara found a strange stone. It was smooth and dull grey, shaped like a fat egg. It felt warm in her hand, even though it had been cold moments before.

"Huh," she mumbled. She absentmindedly pressed the stone against an old, chipped ceramic teacup sitting nearby. A strange feeling washed over her. It was a faint, happy warmth, like a quiet chuckle. It faded quickly.

Elara frowned, confused. Had she imagined it? She tried again, this time touching the stone to an ancient wooden desk. This time, she felt a clear, strong sense of focused work, a bit of worry, then a sigh of relief. It was like a quick movie of emotions played in her mind, linked to the desk.

"No way," Elara whispered. She spent the rest of the afternoon touching the stone to everything in her shop. An old wedding ring gave a powerful wave of love and commitment. A child's worn-out teddy bear filled her with innocent joy, then a pang of sadness. The stone, she realized, let her feel the strongest emotion an object had ever soaked up from its past owners. It was like the objects whispered their deepest feelings to her. She named it the 'Empathy Stone.'

Days turned into weeks. Elara became quite good at sensing the past through her stone. It made her job more interesting, like talking to ghosts without seeing them. One afternoon, a new customer walked in. He carried a small, plain wooden picture frame. It was old and simple, with no picture inside, just clear glass.

"Just cleaning out my grandpa's attic," the man said, placing the frame on the counter. "Figured you might want it."

Elara picked up the frame. It felt light and ordinary. She held the Empathy Stone to its wooden edge, expecting to feel echoes of happy family photos, or maybe the sadness of a lost loved one. She closed her eyes, ready for the whisper of the past.

Instead, a sudden, sharp, overwhelming wave hit her. It wasn't happiness or sadness. It was a deep, chilling sense of *loneliness*. It was so strong it made her gasp, her eyes flying open. Her hand trembled, almost dropping the smooth stone. This was the most powerful feeling she had ever gotten from an object. Why would an old, simple picture frame hold such intense loneliness? It didn't make any sense.

Elara looked at the plain wooden frame, then at the stone in her hand. The feeling of loneliness slowly faded, leaving her puzzled. She brought the frame closer, trying to understand.

### Conclusion
She stared at the empty glass of the picture frame. In its clear surface, she saw her own tired reflection staring back. Her eyes looked a little lost. Then, she looked down at the Empathy Stone, still warm in her hand. A shiver ran down her spine. The stone wasn't showing the frame's past. It was showing her own present. The loneliness hadn't come from the old wood; it had come from the reflection looking back at her. The frame had just been a mirror, and the stone had amplified the quiet ache in her own heart. The Empathy Stone didn't just feel an object's history; it felt the living emotion of the person holding it, reflected by what they saw. And right now, it was Elara's turn to whisper her own story.
