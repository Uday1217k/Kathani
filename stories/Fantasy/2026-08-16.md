# The Taste of Lost Moments

**Genre:** Fantasy | **Date:** 2026-08-16

### Characters
*   **Margo:** A young woman who can turn forgotten memories into edible pebbles.
*   **Old Man Hemlock:** A kind, quiet villager, forgetting his wife's face.
*   **Mayor Finch:** A busy, loud man, forgetting his favorite recipe.

### The Story
In the quiet village of Oakhaven lived Margo. Margo was special. She could make forgotten memories real. Not just her own, but tiny bits of other people's memories, if they were close to slipping away. These memories came to her as small, shiny pebbles, like hard candies, found in dusty corners or under old floorboards. Each pebble had a faint color and smelled vaguely of what it held. A green one might smell of fresh grass, a yellow one of baked apples.

Margo's job was simple. When someone lost a small but important memory, they came to her. She would search through her collection for a matching pebble, or let them pick one she already had. Then, they would just put the pebble on their tongue. It wouldn't melt, but the taste would bring the memory rushing back.

Old Man Hemlock came to Margo often. He was a good man, but time had slowly taken bits of his past. Today, he looked sad. "Margo," he whispered in a rough voice, "I can't see my wife's face anymore. Not clearly. It's just a blurry cloud in my head."
Margo nodded. She searched through her wooden box of pebbles. She found a small, pale blue one that smelled faintly of lavender and old books. "Try this, Mr. Hemlock," she said softly. He put it on his tongue, his eyes closed. A moment later, a tear rolled down his cheek. "Oh, Elara," he whispered, "her smile was just like the morning sun." He thanked Margo with a warm hug and left with a lighter step.

Next, Mayor Finch rushed in. He was a big, loud man, always in a hurry. "Margo, Margo!" he said loudly. "My prize-winning turnip stew recipe! It's gone from my mind! The one I use for the Harvest Festival! I need it!"
Margo sighed. Mayor Finch often lost silly things. She picked out a muddy brown pebble that smelled strongly of root vegetables. "Here, Mayor," she said. He popped it in his mouth. His eyes widened. "Aha! Two sprigs of thyme, not one! I remember!" He patted her on the head and rushed out, shouting orders to his assistant.

Margo loved helping people. It was a good, quiet life. But sometimes, after a busy day, she felt... empty. Like a piece of *her* own memories had been used up to power the pebbles. It was a strange feeling, like a little hum where a clear thought should be. She often found herself forgetting small things, like where she put her keys, or what she had for breakfast. But she always brushed it off. It was just part of the job, she thought.

One evening, after Mayor Finch's visit, Margo sat by her window. The hum of emptiness was stronger than usual. She picked up a small, dull grey pebble from her own pocket. It was one she had found earlier, but hadn't used. It smelled faintly of... nothing. She frowned, confused. Why would she keep a pebble that smelled of nothing? She put it on her tongue, just to see.

### Conclusion
The taste was bland, like plain water, but a chilling realization filled her mind. The pebble didn't taste of nothing. It tasted of *her own* forgotten breakfast. And in that moment, she understood. The pebbles she "found" were never lost memories from others. They were tiny, physical pieces of *her own* memories, pulled from her mind and made solid. Each time she gave a pebble to someone else, a small piece of Margo disappeared, making room for their remembered joy. She wasn't tasting lost moments; she was unknowingly giving away her own.
