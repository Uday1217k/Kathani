# The Grumble Gobbler

**Genre:** Fantasy | **Date:** 2026-09-28

### Characters
Finn: A young man, the village's Grumble Sweeper.
Elder Willow: An old, kind woman from the village.
Forest Creatures: Squirrels, birds, and other small animals.

### The Story
The village of Oakhaven was a bright place, mostly. But if too many people got grumpy, little problems would start. Milk would sour faster, cooking fires would sputter, and socks would often go missing from the wash line. Everyone knew these things happened because of the 'grumbles' – tiny, dark puffs of smoky air that floated out of people's mouths when they sighed too hard or complained about something.

That's where Finn came in. He was the village's Grumble Sweeper. He carried a long, soft brush and a special glass jar. His job was to catch these grumbles before they caused trouble. It was a tricky dance; the grumbles would zip around like tiny bats. Finn would gently sweep them into his jar, which had a snug cork to keep them safe inside. He was very good at it, even though it wasn't a job most people wanted.

When the jar was full, Finn took it to the Ancient Oak. This was a giant tree at the edge of the Whispering Woods, with a big hollow space inside its trunk. Finn would unstopper the jar and pour the grumbles into the dark hollow. A soft, rustling sound would come from deep within the tree, like a sigh. After this, the village would feel lighter for a while, and the little problems would stop. The Ancient Oak itself always looked strong and healthy, its leaves a deep, shining green. Everyone in Oakhaven believed the tree ate the grumbles, making them disappear for good.

One sunny afternoon, Finn was on his way to the Ancient Oak with a jar full of grumbles. Old Man Peterson had been complaining about his aching back all morning, and Mrs. Gable had lost her favorite spoon. The jar felt heavy with all the dark, smoky bits. Finn walked carefully, humming a little tune. But then, a rabbit darted across his path. Finn jumped, his foot slipped on a loose stone, and the glass jar flew from his hand.

It didn't break, but the cork popped out with a loud *thwock*. The grumbles, freed, burst out in a swirling cloud. Finn gasped. He scrambled to put the cork back, but it was too late. A single, stubborn grumble zipped away from the cloud, a tiny dark puff the size of his thumb. It darted towards a squirrel sitting on a low branch. Before Finn could even blink, the grumble shot into the squirrel's ear.

The squirrel froze for a second. Then, its tail puffed out, it let out a furious, chattering squeak, and started angrily kicking nuts off the branch, aiming them right at Finn's head. Finn ducked, surprised. He watched the squirrel stomp its little feet, complaining loudly in squeaks and chitters. Suddenly, Finn understood. The Ancient Oak wasn't eating the grumbles to make them vanish.

### Conclusion
The Ancient Oak was just collecting them. It was a grumble-hoarder. It didn't purify the bad moods; it stored them in its hollow trunk and slowly released them back out, not into the village, but into the small, unsuspecting creatures of the forest. The birds sang more grumpily, the rabbits thumped their feet with more annoyance, and the squirrels were now quite literally kicking nuts at anyone who bothered them. The village felt lighter because the tree wasn't destroying the grumbles, it was simply relocating them, creating a forest full of tiny, bad-tempered mischief-makers. Finn's job wasn't to clean the village, but to feed the tree's secret grumpy army.
