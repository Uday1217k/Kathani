# The Noisy Monsters of Mute Hollow

**Genre:** Fantasy | **Date:** 2026-08-03

### Characters
Finn: A young boy, known for being a bit clumsy and loud.
Granny Elara: Finn's wise and gentle grandmother, who holds old knowledge.
The Villagers of Mute Hollow: Quiet folk who follow tradition.
The Growlers: Mysterious creatures said to come from the forest.

### The Story
The village of Mute Hollow was very quiet. It sat in a small valley, snug between tall, green hills. People there spoke in soft whispers, or often, just used hand signs. Every house had a row of big clay pots by the door. These were not for water or food, but for sounds.

From sunrise to sunset, the villagers caught sounds. A mother’s hum, a dog’s bark, the thump of an axe hitting wood – every single noise, big or small, had to be scooped up and put into a clay pot. Special wooden lids with tight seals kept the sounds inside. The villagers believed this protected them from the Growlers. Growlers were scary beasts, they said. They lived in the Shifting Woods outside the valley. They hunted by sound and shook the ground when they moved. If too much noise got out of Mute Hollow, the Growlers would come and smash everything.

Finn was a boy of ten, and he found it hard to be quiet. He loved the crunch of dry leaves under his boots. He loved the splash of water when he jumped into the stream. Most of all, he loved to laugh out loud, a big, belly-shaking laugh that always got him a gentle frown from his Granny Elara. He often got into trouble for letting a cough get too loud or dropping a basket with a clatter.

One sunny afternoon, Finn was helping Granny Elara put away the day's sounds. He had a small clay jar full of bird chirps and a few giggles from his cousin. Granny held a larger pot, heavy with the sounds of cooking and distant sheep. The sun was dipping low, painting the sky orange and pink. The sunset bell, a small chime rung only at the very last moment, would ring soon. That was the signal to seal all pots.

Finn walked to the shelf where the pots stood, tall and round. He moved quickly, a bit too quickly. His foot caught on a loose stone. He tripped! His jar flew from his hand, spinning through the air. It hit Granny’s pot with a loud CLANG! Both jars tumbled to the ground. They broke into many pieces. The sounds rushed out like a wild, noisy wind. The loud CLANG! of the pots breaking mixed with bird chirps, giggles, sheep bleats, and all the cooking noises. It was a roar of noise, louder than anything Finn had ever heard in Mute Hollow.

Then the sunset bell chimed, soft and late.

Panic spread through the village. People pointed to the edge of the Shifting Woods. The ground began to rumble, a low, deep growl. "The Growlers!" someone whispered, a rare loud sound. Houses shook. From the edge of the woods, a wavy, shimmering shape appeared. It wasn't a beast with teeth or claws. It was a huge, blurry cloud of color and movement. It roared, but not with a monster's roar. It sounded like a thousand sounds all mixed up and magnified – a giant, vibrating mess of noise. It rushed towards the village, rumbling loudly.

Finn was frozen, scared silly. But Granny Elara, instead of hiding, just stood there, watching the noisy, shapeless thing come closer. It didn't attack. It just swirled and roared, then bumped into a fence, making it shake. It looked more like a giant, angry dust storm than a hungry beast.

"They're not trying to hurt us, child," Granny Elara said, her voice calm despite the shaking ground. "They're just trying to get free."

The giant, swirling thing bumped into the old oak tree, then turned and began to rush out of the valley, moving fast towards the open fields beyond the hills. As it went, it seemed to break apart. Smaller, wavier shapes spun off it. The rumbling became less. The loud, mixed-up noise slowly faded into what sounded like a strong wind. After a few minutes, the valley was quiet again, but in a different way. It felt… lighter.

### Conclusion
Granny Elara then told Finn and the other villagers. "The Growlers are not monsters from the woods. They are the sounds we trap. When we keep them locked in our pots, they get angry and big. They become wild. When too many break free at once, they rush out, all confused and loud, looking for a way to turn back into normal sounds in the wide world. We thought we were keeping danger out, but we were just making our own big noise monsters." From that day on, the people of Mute Hollow slowly learned to let sounds be free. The village became a little less quiet, but the 'Growlers' never came back.
