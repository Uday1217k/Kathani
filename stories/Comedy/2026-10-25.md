# The Uninvited Vegetable

**Genre:** Comedy | **Date:** 2026-10-25

### Characters
*   Mildred Putter: A very organized woman, proud of her pristine garden plot.
*   Gary Thistle: Mildred's relaxed, easygoing neighbour at the community garden.

### The Story
Mildred Putter surveyed her community garden plot with immense satisfaction. Every row was straight, every lettuce leaf crisp, every tomato plant supported by its own sturdy stake. Her plot, Number 7B, was a masterpiece of order. She often glanced at Gary Thistle's plot, 7C, next door. Gary’s patch was a bit… wild. Things grew where they pleased, often leaning into Mildred’s space. It was a constant quiet battle, mostly fought by Mildred moving Gary’s stray pumpkin vines back to his side of the line.

One Tuesday morning, Mildred’s perfect world wobbled. Nestled between her prize-winning carrots and her precisely spaced radishes, a new plant had appeared. It wasn’t a carrot. It wasn’t a radish. It wasn’t anything Mildred recognized. It had broad, ugly leaves, rough to the touch, and a stem that looked like a knobbly green fist pushing through the soil. It was definitely not supposed to be there.

“A weed,” Mildred declared to herself, pulling on her thick gardening gloves. She grabbed the stem and pulled. Nothing. The plant held on with the tenacity of a barnacle. Mildred grunted, pulled harder, her face turning a faint shade of plum. The plant didn’t budge.

Over the next few days, the plant grew. And grew. It shot up, unfurling more of its strange, coarse leaves. It started to look less like a weed and more like something that belonged in a science fiction movie. Mildred tried everything. She sprayed it with vinegar, then with a concoction of soap and water. The plant merely seemed to glisten more, growing bigger and more defiant. She even tried talking to it, sternly, telling it to leave. It ignored her.

Gary, meanwhile, would wander by, watering his chaotic patch, often humming off-key. He’d cast a casual eye over Mildred’s increasingly monstrous intruder. “Quite a specimen you got there, Mildred,” he’d say, a small smile playing on his lips.

Mildred, suspicious, once asked, “You haven’t, by any chance, dropped anything into my plot recently, Gary?”

Gary scratched his head. “Me? Nah. Always careful. You know me, Mildred. Mr. Precise.” This was a blatant lie, and they both knew it. Gary once mistook a trowel for a spade.

The plant continued its ascent. It developed what looked like fat, green, lumpy fruit hanging from its thick vines. They were the color of pond scum and shaped like misshapen footballs. Other gardeners started to stop and stare. Mrs. Henderson from Plot 8A, a notoriously gossipy woman, even suggested it might be a rare, invasive species that would take over the entire garden if Mildred didn’t act fast.

Mildred decided drastic action was needed. She borrowed a small pickaxe from her shed. This plant was coming out, roots and all. As she raised the tool, ready to strike, Gary sauntered over.

“Whoa there, Mildred!” he said, reaching out a hand. “What are you doing to poor Cyril?”

Mildred lowered the pickaxe slowly. “Cyril? This… this *thing* has a name?”

Gary chuckled, walking over to the plant. He gently plucked one of the lumpy green footballs. “Sure does. My Grandma’s prize-winning ‘Cyril’s Ugly Wonder Gourd.’ Tastes like a dream, but you won’t find it in any seed catalogue. She just called it Cyril because it looked like her grumpy old cat.” He wiped a bit of dirt off the gourd. “I was just trying to grow some of the seeds she gave me. Dropped one over the fence a few weeks back. Didn’t think it would sprout, especially not with your military-grade tidiness.” He paused, inspecting the large, rough leaf. “Looks like it thrived, actually. Want to try some? Makes a fantastic soup.”

### Conclusion
Mildred stared from the pickaxe to the gourd in Gary’s hand, then to her meticulously ordered carrot patch now overshadowed by Cyril. Gary then bent down and pointed to a similar, smaller plant struggling to grow in his own wildly overgrown plot. "See? Mine barely made it. Yours is magnificent!" Mildred groaned, realizing she'd spent weeks battling what was essentially a delicious, rare vegetable, and that Gary had just complimented her green thumb.
