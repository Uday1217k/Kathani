# The Case of Spot Number Seven

**Genre:** Comedy | **Date:** 2026-11-08

### Characters
Arthur Pringle: A very neat man, proud of his blue sedan and his parking spot.
Mrs. Gable: Arthur's friendly, observant neighbor.
Mr. Henderson: The quiet building manager.

### The Story
Arthur Pringle loved his blue sedan. He loved it even more when it was parked perfectly in his assigned spot, number seven, behind his small apartment building. Every morning, he’d check it from his kitchen window. Clean, safe, right where it should be. It was a good start to his day.

Then, one Tuesday, the spot was taken. Not by his car, but by a dented, beige minivan. Arthur frowned. He left a polite note under the wiper blade. "Excuse me, this is Private Parking Spot #7. Please move your vehicle." The next day, the minivan was gone. Arthur smiled. Problem solved.

But it wasn't. The minivan came back. And then a silver hatchback. And then a red pickup truck. Spot number seven became a free-for-all, a game of musical cars that only Arthur was losing. His polite notes grew less polite. His forehead grew more wrinkled. He started parking his blue sedan in visitor spots, which felt wrong and made him worry about getting towed.

Arthur decided he needed a plan. A clever, foolproof plan. First, he bought a bright yellow sign. It read: "RESERVED. SPOT #7. UNAUTHORISED VEHICLES WILL BE TOWED." He bolted it to the fence right above the spot. He felt a surge of triumph. Surely, this would work.

It did not. Two days later, a muddy, green SUV sat proudly in spot number seven. Arthur was steaming. He stomped outside and stared at the sign. It was right there! Clear as day! This was a direct challenge.

His next move was bigger. He ordered a fake security camera online. It was a simple, grey dome with a blinking red light. He got a small ladder and fixed it to the drainpipe, pointing directly at spot number seven. He even bought a neon yellow steering wheel lock, not because he feared theft, but to make his sedan look very, very important. When he parked his car in spot seven now, it looked like a high-security vehicle in a highly monitored spot. He felt like a genius.

For a whole week, spot number seven stayed empty. Arthur was ecstatic. He’d check from his window, a proud grin on his face. No minivan, no hatchback, no muddy SUV. His plan had worked! He felt a sense of peace he hadn't known in months. His morning coffee tasted better. His blue sedan gleamed under the sun, undisturbed. He even waved to Mrs. Gable, his neighbor, who was watering her potted plants. "Looks like you finally sorted out that parking trouble, Arthur," she called out. He just nodded, beaming.

Then, on Monday afternoon, there was a knock on his door. It was Mr. Henderson, the building manager. Mr. Henderson was a quiet man, always fixing things. He held a clipboard and had a kind, tired look on his face.

"Arthur," Mr. Henderson began, a small smile playing on his lips. "I need to talk to you about the parking."

Arthur puffed out his chest. "Ah, yes, Mr. Henderson. I think I've finally put a stop to those pesky interlopers. My new security measures seem to have done the trick."

Mr. Henderson nodded slowly. "Yes, they certainly have. The sign is quite... noticeable. And the camera. Very persuasive." He paused, then pointed to a line item on his clipboard. "The thing is, Arthur, your assigned spot is actually number seventeen. You know, the one right next to the big oak tree?"

### Conclusion
Arthur's jaw dropped. Spot seventeen? Not seven? He stared blankly at Mr. Henderson. "But... but it's always been number seven!" Mr. Henderson chuckled softly. "No, Arthur. Number seven belongs to Mrs. Gable. She's been parking in visitor spots for weeks, too polite to say anything about your 'security measures' in her spot. And the minivan? That's mine. I needed to use Mrs. Gable's spot for a few days while I patched a leak right outside my own spot, number six." Arthur felt his face turn a deep, burning red. All those notes, the sign, the fake camera... all for the wrong spot.
