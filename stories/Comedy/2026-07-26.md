# The Curbside Conundrum

**Genre:** Comedy | **Date:** 2026-07-26

### Characters
*   Arthur: A man in his late forties, creative but easily flustered.
*   Brenda: Arthur's wife, practical and patient, but with limits.
*   Mittens: Mrs. Henderson's ginger cat, a master of silent judgment.

### The Story
Arthur stared at the green wheelie bin at the end of his driveway. It was trash day, and the bin was full to bursting. Its lid yawned open, revealing a mountain of old cereal boxes and banana peels. Brenda, his wife, had given him the look. The look meant, "That bin isn't going to roll itself to the curb, Arthur."

Arthur sighed. Taking out the trash was his personal Everest. Not the act of *taking* it out, mind you, but the act of *approaching* it. He had tried many ways to solve this weekly challenge.

Last week, he’d fashioned a long pole with a grabber claw at the end. He’d leaned out the upstairs window, stretching for the bin, only for the pole to snap in half. The bin just sat there, smug. Two weeks before that, he tried to train their dog, Pipsqueak, to nudge it. Pipsqueak, a fluffy terrier mix, sniffed the bin once, sneezed, and then ran off to chase a leaf.

Today, Arthur had a new plan. He put on his heavy gardening gloves and a ski mask. He reasoned that if he couldn't see the bin's "eyes," it couldn't see his. Brenda watched him from the kitchen window, stirring her coffee slowly. He marched down the driveway, a man on a mission. He reached out a gloved hand, paused, took a deep breath, and then quickly grabbed the bin handle. He yanked it forward. The bin didn't move an inch. He pulled harder. Still nothing. He looked down. The wheels were stuck in a small patch of mud. He tried again, grunting. The bin tilted, then toppled over with a crash, spilling its contents across the lawn. A half-eaten sandwich rolled lazily onto the pavement.

Brenda opened the front door. "Arthur?" she called, her voice calm but firm. "What on earth are you doing?"

Arthur pulled off his ski mask, his face red. "It's stuck, Brenda! It's like it's glued to the ground!"

"Or maybe," Brenda said, walking towards the mess, "you're just making it harder than it needs to be." She bent down, grabbed the bin handle with her bare hand, and with a single, easy push, rolled it free of the mud. Then she righted the bin and started picking up the scattered trash. "Just roll it to the curb, Arthur. It's not rocket science."

Arthur watched her, feeling small. He knew she was right. It wasn't hard. It was just... difficult for *him*. He finally took the handle and, with a quick sprint, pushed the bin the last few feet to the curb. He let go as if the handle was hot, and hurried back inside, pretending to be very busy wiping down the kitchen counter.

### Conclusion
Later that evening, after the trash truck had done its noisy rounds, Brenda found Arthur polishing the toaster with unusual vigor. "Okay, Arthur," she said, leaning against the counter. "Spill it. Why do you make such a fuss about the trash bin? It's just a bin." Arthur stopped polishing, his shoulders slumped. He looked around, as if checking for spies. "It's... it's Mittens," he whispered, pointing a shaky finger towards Mrs. Henderson's house across the street. "That cat. Every trash day, it sits on the lid. And it just... stares at me. Like it knows all my secrets. Like it's judging my choice of yogurt container. I swear, sometimes it even gives me a little evil blink." Brenda stared at him for a moment, then burst out laughing, a loud, clear sound that made Arthur jump. "Arthur," she managed between giggles, "Mittens is a lazy, old cat. It probably just likes the warm plastic of the bin lid." Arthur remained unconvinced. "You didn't see the glare, Brenda. You just didn't see the *glare*."
