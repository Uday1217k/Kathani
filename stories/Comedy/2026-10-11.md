# The Great Rubber Chicken Mystery

**Genre:** Comedy | **Date:** 2026-10-11

### Characters
Clara: An ordinary woman who values order and quiet.
Gary: A post office clerk, very strict about rules and regulations.
Leo: Clara's friend, a bit of a jokester.
Aunt Mildred: Clara's eccentric aunt (appears only in the twist).

### The Story
Clara enjoyed a quiet life. Her biggest surprise on most days was finding a matching pair of socks. So, when a medium-sized box appeared on her doorstep, addressed clearly to her, she felt a small thrill. Maybe it was that new coffee maker she'd been eyeing, or a book she'd forgotten she ordered.

She tore open the tape. Inside, nestled in a sea of bubble wrap, was a single, bright yellow, very large, and frankly, quite unsettling rubber chicken. It had wide, painted eyes and a beak that looked permanently ready to squawk. Clara stared at it. She did not own a rubber chicken. She had never *wanted* a rubber chicken.

She checked the label again. "Clara Jenkins, 14 Maple Street." No mistake there. She flipped the box over, looking for a return address or any hint of a sender. Nothing but a generic online retailer sticker. "Well," she sighed, "this is going back."

The next morning, armed with the offending fowl, Clara marched to the post office. Gary, the clerk, peered at her over his glasses. "What can I do for you, Clara?" he asked, his voice as flat as an old pancake.

"Gary," Clara began, pushing the box across the counter. The chicken's head wobbled precariously. "I received this. It's addressed to me, but I didn't order it. I need to return it."

Gary picked up the box, gave the rubber chicken a wary look, and then put it down. "Did it come with a return label, Clara?"

"No, Gary. That's the problem. Just my address."

"And a sender?"

"Just a generic retailer. No specific company name."

Gary tapped his pen on the counter. "Well, Clara, if it's addressed to you, and there's no sender details, and no return code... then it's yours. We can't accept returns without a clear point of origin or an authorized return slip."

Clara blinked. "But it's a rubber chicken, Gary! I don't want a rubber chicken!"

Gary shrugged. "Not my department, Clara. Rules are rules." He gave the chicken another suspicious glance. "Maybe it's a gift."

Defeated, Clara took the chicken home. It sat on her kitchen counter, a silent, yellow judgment. Her cat, Mittens, regarded it with extreme caution, occasionally batting at its feet before scampering away.

That evening, Clara called her friend, Leo. "You will not believe what happened," she groaned, recounting the saga of the unsolicited poultry.

Leo laughed. "A rubber chicken? That's amazing! Maybe it's a new pet."

"It's not funny, Leo. I'm stuck with it. Gary at the post office was useless."

"Hmm," Leo mused. "You know, sometimes those novelty things have... secrets. Did you check inside?"

Clara paused. "Inside? What, like, open it up?"

"Well, yeah. Sometimes there's a plug at the bottom, or you might have to snip it. You know, like an Easter egg, but a chicken."

Clara hung up, a strange mix of hope and annoyance bubbling inside her. She picked up the rubber chicken. Its head still wobbled. There was no obvious plug. With a sigh, she grabbed a small pair of scissors and carefully, tentatively, made a small cut along the bottom seam of the rubber.

### Conclusion
Clara carefully unrolled the paper. Her eyes scanned the familiar handwriting. It read: "Happy 40th Birthday! I told you I'd send something *egg-cellent* to cluck you up! Love, Aunt Mildred." Clara stared at the note, then at the now-sliced rubber chicken. She burst out laughing, a genuine, loud laugh that even made Mittens jump. Aunt Mildred, always full of surprises, and apparently, always on time for Clara's birthday, which was, in fact, tomorrow. And Clara had completely forgotten.
