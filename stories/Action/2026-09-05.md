# The Durian Run

**Genre:** Action | **Date:** 2026-09-05

### Characters
Leo: A hardworking bicycle courier, known for his speed and reliability.
Mr. Chen: An eccentric, well-known food critic.
Big Guy and Slim: Two thugs working for a rival chef.

### The Story
The phone buzzed. Leo picked it up, still half-asleep. "Leo's Deliveries," he mumbled. A sharp voice on the other end gave him an address and a job. "Highly critical component. Must be at the old lighthouse by 6 AM. No exceptions. Pay is... generous." Leo blinked. The sun wasn't even up yet. A lighthouse? This was weird, but the money sounded good. He pulled on his gear, grabbed his bike, and rode to the pickup spot.

The client was just a shadow in the dark alley. A small, sturdy box was thrust into his hands. "This is it. Guard it with your life." Leo strapped the box securely into his backpack and pushed off. The city was just starting to stir. He cut through empty streets, his bike humming. He felt good, fast.

Then he saw it. A black van, dark windows, pulling out from a side street behind him. It wasn't driving normally. It sped up, closing the gap. Leo's gut tightened. He swerved down a narrow lane, a shortcut he knew well. The van tried to follow but scraped its side on a wall, forced to go around.

Leo pedaled harder, weaving through a public park, scattering early joggers. He glanced back. The van was back on his tail, closer now. He took a sharp turn into a market alley, full of early vendors setting up. Carts, boxes, people. The van couldn't fit. Two big guys jumped out instead. One was burly, the other tall and thin.

"Hey, you! Stop!" the burly one yelled.

Leo didn't stop. He ducked under a hanging tarp, swerved around a fruit stand, almost hitting a pile of apples. The big guys were surprisingly fast on foot, knocking over a display of fresh bread as they chased him. Leo saw a gap between two buildings, too tight for anyone but him and his bike. He squeezed through, scraping his knees, but he was free again. He heard angry shouts fade behind him.

His heart thumped. They really wanted this package. What was in it? He didn't know, and didn't want to think about it. He just had to get it to the lighthouse. He checked his watch. 5:45 AM. Not much time left. He pushed himself, legs burning, through the last stretch of city and onto the coast road.

The old lighthouse stood tall against the pale morning sky. He saw a figure waiting at the base. Mr. Chen. He had seen his picture in magazines; a famous food critic known for his picky tastes. Leo pulled up, breathless, covered in dust, a scratch on his cheek.

"Right on time," Mr. Chen said, a small smile on his face. "The package?"

Leo unstrapped his backpack with shaking hands and carefully handed over the small box. It was a little dented on one corner, but still intact.

### Conclusion
Mr. Chen took the box and, with surprising care, opened it. Inside, nestled in thick padding, was a single, large, spiky fruit. A durian. It looked perfectly ripe. "Magnificent!" Mr. Chen exclaimed, ignoring Leo's confused stare. "Just in time for the live broadcast. My rivals tried to stop it, you see. They knew I had sourced the finest durian in the world for my breakfast show this morning. Its perfect ripeness window is only a few minutes long. Without it, my whole segment would be ruined!" He pulled out a fat wad of cash. "You saved the show, Leo. And my reputation. Thank you." Leo, still smelling the faint, sweet, and oddly stinky fruit, just nodded, took the money, and tried to make sense of the wildest delivery run of his life for a single piece of fruit.
