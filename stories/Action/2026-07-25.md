# The Wrong Drop

**Genre:** Action | **Date:** 2026-07-25

### Characters
*   Leo: A bike courier, fast and clever.
*   Mr. Hayes: A stern man in a dark suit, leader of the 'pursuers'.
*   The Team: Other people working with Mr. Hayes.

### The Story
Leo pedaled hard, weaving through the morning city traffic. Another day, another stack of envelopes and small boxes. He worked for "Swift Spokes," a bicycle delivery service. Today's special item was a small, plain cardboard box, light in his insulated bag. It felt like it held a small plant pot, maybe a fancy herb or a tiny succulent. "Probably for some office desk," he thought, zipping past a slow-moving bus.

He was heading to the financial district, an area full of tall glass buildings. That's when he first saw it: a sleek black car, moving too fast for the street, trying to get close to him. Leo ignored it at first. City drivers were crazy. But then, the car cut sharply into his lane. Leo swerved, nearly hitting a parked taxi. His heart gave a hard thump. He glanced back. The driver, a man in a dark suit, was staring straight at him. His face was serious, almost angry.

"What's his problem?" Leo muttered, pushing harder on his pedals. He knew every back alley, every shortcut, every brick archway in this part of town. He ducked into a narrow path between two buildings, usually only for trash cans. The black car couldn't follow. Leo grinned, thinking he'd lost the weird driver. But as he shot out onto another street, a second black car was waiting, blocking the road. This one had another person inside, looking just as stern.

Panic started to bubble in Leo's stomach. They weren't just angry drivers. They wanted *him*. Or, more likely, they wanted the package. What could be in a plain little box that made people act like this? Drugs? Some super rare, illegal flower? Leo just wanted to finish his delivery and go home. He didn't sign up for this. He slammed on his brakes, spinning his bike around, and pedaled against traffic, yelling at confused drivers. The cars couldn't follow easily in the dense lanes.

He zipped through a pedestrian zone, dodging people with quick turns. A flash of black in his mirror showed the suit-wearing man, Mr. Hayes, now running on foot, surprisingly fast, shouting something Leo couldn't make out. Leo didn't stop to listen. He needed to get this package to its address. He looked at the label: 17B Sterling Tower. Almost there. He pushed his bike up a curb, flying across a small park, sending pigeons scattering. The black cars were behind him again, honking loudly, trying to close in.

With one last burst of speed, Leo reached Sterling Tower. He skidded to a halt, locked his bike, and ran inside. He took the elevator up, panting, the small box clutched tight. He found Suite 17B, a door marked "Bio-Solutions Group." He knocked, then pressed the buzzer. The door opened. A kind-looking woman with bright green glasses smiled at him. "Swift Spokes? You have a delivery?"

Leo handed her the box. "Yeah, here you go. And tell your bosses they should use a different shipping method. I just got chased halfway across the city for this thing."

### Conclusion
Just as the woman took the box, the elevator doors dinged open. Mr. Hayes and his team rushed out, faces tight with worry. They saw the box in the woman's hands. "You have it!" Mr. Hayes gasped, relief washing over his face. He looked at Leo. "You! Why didn't you stop? We were trying to help you! That's the last surviving specimen of the Mirandula Bloom! A single bump, a wrong temperature, and it could have died! We sent our fastest team to ensure its safe transfer, but you picked it up by mistake instead of our dedicated courier! We were just trying to guide you and keep it safe from all the city jostling!" Leo stared, jaw open. "The last... what?" The woman gently hugged the box. "It's a very special plant, Leo. Thank you for getting it here. And for the wild ride, I suppose."
