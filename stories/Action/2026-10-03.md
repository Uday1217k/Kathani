# The Card Trick

**Genre:** Action | **Date:** 2026-10-03

### Characters
Jax: A quick-thinking, agile freelance operator, good at getting things.
The Client: An anonymous voice on a secure phone.
Factory Guards: Standard, slightly clumsy security personnel.
The Collectors: A small group of well-dressed, intense individuals.

### The Story
The phone buzzed. Jax picked it up. A low voice spoke, clear but muffled. "Corner of Elm and Baker. Old brick factory. Third floor, behind the main office safe. Black briefcase. No funny business. Just get it." The voice hung up. Jax knew the drill. No names, no faces, just a task and a payment that always cleared.

He drove his beat-up car to the factory. It was dark, quiet, just a few security lights humming. Jax hopped a low wall, landing softly. He moved like a shadow, checking doors, finding a loose window on the side. Inside, the air smelled of old dust and oil. He found the stairs, took them two at a time, moving fast but silent.

On the third floor, a single guard sat at a desk, looking bored. Jax took a deep breath. He tossed a small pebble at a metal sheet a few aisles over. CLANG! The guard jumped, pulling out his phone. "What was that?" he mumbled, walking towards the sound. Jax slipped past the desk, found the main office, and the big, old safe. He pulled out a slim tool kit. In minutes, he heard the click. The heavy door swung open. Inside, a single black briefcase sat on a shelf.

He grabbed it. Just as he turned, the guard was back. "Hey! You!"
Jax didn't stop. He sprinted, the briefcase tucked tight under his arm. The guard yelled, "Intruder!" and hit an alarm button. A loud, ringing bell filled the factory. Jax found another window, kicked it open, and jumped. He rolled on the ground, coming up on his feet without missing a beat.

Two more guards were running from the front gate. Jax darted between parked trucks, cutting through a dark alley. He heard car tires squealing behind him. He didn't look back. He ran across a main street, dodging cars, then into a busy market. People shouted, stalls tipped. He pushed past, weaving through the crowd. He was fast, faster than the guards, who were now struggling to keep up.

He ducked into a small coffee shop, blending in with the late-night crowd. He ordered a coffee, paid with cash, and calmly walked out the back door. He was in a quiet residential area now. The sirens and shouts faded. He had lost them. He found a dark bench under a streetlamp. He took a breath, the thrill of the chase slowly leaving his veins. He looked at the briefcase. It felt light, but solid. He wondered what was inside. Money? Jewels? Secret papers?

### Conclusion
Jax clicked the latches. The briefcase opened. He peered inside. There was no stack of cash, no shimmering diamonds. Instead, carefully placed in foam cutouts, were dozens of small, laminated cards. They were from an old trading card game, "Mythic Beasts," popular decades ago. One card, right in the center, was clearly the prize. It showed a winged, fire-breathing creature, its surface shimmering. It was in perfect, mint condition, still sealed in its original plastic casing. A small note tucked beside it read: "The Phoenix Ascendant. Edition 1. Thank you, Jax. Payment sent. Client 7." Jax stared at the card. He had just dodged bullets and outrun guards for a piece of cardboard. Then he remembered the news story he'd seen last week about a rare "Mythic Beasts" card selling for millions. He closed the briefcase with a soft click and shook his head, a small smile playing on his lips. "Well," he muttered to himself, "that was a tangy flavor of crazy."
