# Cloud Runner

**Genre:** Action | **Date:** 2026-08-14

### Characters
Lena: A skilled, quick package runner.
Kai: Another top package runner, Lena's rival.
Client: The person who ordered the special package.

### The Story
The message flashed in Lena's view. "Platinum Rush. Morning Cloud Croissant. Urgent." Her eyes narrowed. This was the big one. A "Cloud Croissant" meant a delivery from *Puff's Bakery*, a place known for pastries that stayed perfect for only seven minutes after leaving the oven. This kind of job paid big, but it demanded everything.

Lena strapped her pack tighter. She checked her route on the city map that glowed on her wrist. The bakery was seven blocks away, across three rooftops and through the tangled market district. She had to move.

She burst from her building, hitting the street in a sprint. People moved out of her way, knowing the look of a 'Rush' runner. Lena ran, her feet light. She saw the first obstacle: a tall, metal fence. No problem. She leaped, found a foothold, and pulled herself up, scrambling over in a blur.

Up on the first rooftop, the wind bit at her. A gap yawned between buildings. It was a wide jump, but Lena didn't slow. She ran to the edge, springed, and sailed across, landing softly on the next roof. She rolled to absorb the impact, then was up and running again.

Automated security drones, shaped like hovering metal balls, zipped past, scanning for rule-breakers. Lena ducked behind a ventilation unit, then burst out, weaving through air ducts and pipes. One drone got too close, its red light blinking. Lena slid under a low beam, losing it.

Then she saw him. Kai. Another top runner, known for his reckless speed. He was on a parallel building, going for the same target, his face set. A fierce smile touched Lena's lips. This wasn't just a delivery anymore. It was a race.

They hit the market district almost at the same time. The narrow paths were packed with people, stalls spilling with goods. Kai pushed through, bumping shoppers aside. Lena was smoother. She jumped over a fruit stand, balanced on a precarious pile of boxes, and dodged a flying blanket of scarves. She saw an opening, a narrow alleyway between two towering food carts. She squeezed through, barely fitting, coming out a step ahead of Kai.

Her legs burned, her lungs ached, but the clock on her wrist kept ticking down: "00:03:10." Not much time. The bakery was now just one more rooftop away. A construction site blocked her usual path. A crane arm, heavy with a steel beam, swung slowly above her.

Lena made a quick decision. Risky, but fast. She sprinted towards the moving beam, timed her jump, and grabbed onto the rough metal as it swung. Her muscles screamed, but she held on, letting it carry her across the final gap. She dropped off onto the bakery roof, landing with a thump.

She was there. She slid down a fire escape, hit the ground running, and burst through the back door of Puff's Bakery. The baker, a round man with flour on his apron, held out a small, warm package. "Right on time, Lena!" he puffed.

Lena grabbed the package, careful not to crush it. She scanned her next destination: the client's penthouse, just three blocks away. Almost done. She ran, finding a clear path, her speed not faltering.

### Conclusion
She reached the grand lobby of the penthouse tower, flashed her runner ID, and took the express lift. On the top floor, a door opened. A woman, sharp-eyed and dressed in fine clothes, stood waiting. Lena held out the small package. The clock on her wrist clicked: "00:00:15." Fifteen seconds to spare. The woman took the package gently. She opened it. Inside, nestled on a soft cloth, was a single, golden, perfectly baked Morning Cloud Croissant, still warm, steam barely rising. It was the only item in the entire package. The woman sighed happily, a look of pure joy on her face. "Perfect," she whispered. "Absolutely perfect." Lena just nodded, sweat dripping down her face. All that for a single, warm pastry. And it was worth every leap, every dodge, every sprint.
