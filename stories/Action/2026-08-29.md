# The Old Clock's Run

**Genre:** Action | **Date:** 2026-08-29

### Characters
Martha: An elderly woman, owner of a small shop, sharp and quick.
Leo: Martha's young grandson, fast and eager.

### The Story
Martha’s shop was a quiet place, full of old things that smelled of dust and time. She sat on her stool, reading a newspaper, glasses perched on her nose. Outside, the city hummed. Today was special. On the counter, a small, shiny clock ticked loudly. It was no bigger than Martha’s hand, made of brass and dark wood, with tiny gears you could see working. It had arrived just this morning.

The shop bell jingled. A young man, Leo, stepped inside. He had a backpack slung over one shoulder and a cap pulled low. Martha looked up, a small smile on her face. Leo met her eyes, a spark of challenge in his own. Without a word, he grabbed the little brass clock from the counter. Then he ran.

Martha didn't call out. She put her newspaper down, pushed her glasses up, and moved. She was out the door before the bell stopped jingling. Leo was already far ahead, weaving through the morning shoppers. Martha didn't sprint like Leo. She moved with a quick, steady pace, her old sneakers making soft sounds on the pavement.

Leo cut into a narrow alleyway between a bakery and a hardware store. Martha knew that alley. She saw a gap between two bins and squeezed through, coming out onto a different street ahead of him. Leo looked back, panting, and saw her. His eyes went wide. He sped up, darting into the busy market square.

The market was a maze of bright stalls and noisy chatter. Leo pushed past a man selling big bags of fruit, making apples roll everywhere. Martha didn't stop. She saw a tall stack of empty boxes and used them to leap over a low fence, landing lightly on the other side. She wove through people, careful not to bump anyone, but always keeping Leo in sight. He glanced back again, his face now a mix of surprise and worry.

He ran towards the river, thinking he could lose her in the park. Martha smiled. She knew the park better than anyone. She cut through a patch of thick bushes, came out near the old fountain, and saw Leo struggling to climb a small hill. He reached the top, looked around, and his shoulders sagged. Martha was already there, leaning against a tree, barely breathing hard.

Leo slumped to the ground, tired. "How... how did you do that?" he puffed. The little brass clock was still clutched tight in his hand.

### Conclusion
Martha walked over and held out her hand. Leo gave her the clock. She took it, turning it over in her fingers. "You're fast, Leo. Very fast." She smiled, a real, warm smile this time. "But you forgot the most important rule of a good courier: know your city better than your own pockets." She tapped the brass clock. "This wasn't just any old clock, was it? This was your final test. And you passed." Leo looked up, confused. "Passed what?" Martha winked. "You're ready. That big package, the one they need delivered by sunset? You'll take it." Leo's jaw dropped. The "stolen" clock had been a trick all along, a clever test set by his grandma, the boss of the city's best, most secret delivery service.
